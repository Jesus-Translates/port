# Curriculum gap analysis — us vs Practice Portuguese

Source archive: `content/competitor-pp-curriculum.json` (captured 2026-08-08, logged-in paid
account, read-only). Our syllabus: `content/syllabus-{a1,a2,b1,b2}.json`.

| | A1 | A2 | B1 | B2 | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Practice Portuguese | 39 | 55 | 24 | 19 | **137** |
| Us | 32 | 44 | 28 | 22 | **126** |

Shape difference: they put 69% of their course in A1+A2; we put 60%. They taper hard after
A2 (43 units across B1+B2); we keep 50. Their curve is a beginner-acquisition funnel. Ours
is a resident's curve — and for this family that is the right shape.

One structural finding worth stating up front: **PP has no "Grammar Practice" category.**
Their taxonomy is exactly three labels (Communication / Grammar / Vocabulary). They have no
consolidation or contrast units anywhere in 137 units. Our `grammar-practice` category
(`perfeito-ou-imperfeito`, `condicional-na-pratica`, `onde-por-o-pronome`,
`infinitivo-pessoal-e-conjuntivo`, `discurso-indireto-no-passado`, `estar-a-infinitive`,
`ir-plus-infinitive-future`) has no counterpart in their course at all. That is our single
biggest structural advantage and it should not be traded away.

## 2. What THEY have that WE lack — ranked by what this family would actually miss

Ranked by how often a family living near Torres Vedras would hit the gap in real life.

1. **The verb `ficar`** (they: A2.53). The single most important omission. `Ficar` is
   location (*a farmácia fica ao lado*), becoming (*fiquei doente*), staying (*ficámos duas
   noites*), arrangements (*ficamos assim*), and judgement (*fica-te bem*). It is
   unavoidable in every conversation and it does not map to one English verb, so it will
   never be acquired incidentally. → **Add at A2: "O verbo ficar".**

2. **Airport and flying** (they: A2.49). A family living in Portugal with relatives abroad
   is in an airport several times a year: check-in, bagagem de porão, porta de embarque,
   atraso, ligação perdida, excesso de peso. We have `transportes` and `fazer-uma-viagem`
   but neither covers the airport. → **Add at A2: "No aeroporto e no avião".**

3. **Negation as an explicit unit** (they: A1.5). We never teach negation directly. European
   Portuguese requires the double negative (*não vi ninguém*, *não quero nada*, *nunca lá
   fui*), which English speakers reliably get wrong for months. Currently a learner has to
   infer the pattern. → **Add at A1: "Dizer que não".**

4. **Christmas, New Year and Reis** (they: B1.24). We have Carnaval and the Oeste festivals
   but nothing for the biggest family season of the year — consoada, bacalhau, bolo-rei,
   Boas Festas, Feliz Ano Novo, Reis on 6 January. This is the moment neighbours and school
   most expect the family to participate. → **Add at B1: "Natal, Ano Novo e os Reis".**

5. **Cognate patterns as an explicit strategy** (they: A2.8). Teaching the mappings
   (-tion→-ção, -ty→-dade, -ly→-mente, -ment→-mento) is the cheapest vocabulary multiplier
   in the course — hundreds of words for one unit. We teach `falsos-amigos` at B1 but never
   the productive side. → **Add at A2: "Cognatos".**

6. **Tools, electricity and breakdowns** (they: A2.5 "Coisas 1", B1.1 "Coisas 2"). Vocabulary
   for wiring, fuses, machinery, tools and things that break. We have
   `chamar-um-canalizador` but the learner has no nouns to use in it — they can open the
   call and not name the problem. → **Add at B1: "Ferramentas, eletricidade e avarias".**

7. **Discourse markers taught early** (they: B1.3; we bury them at B2.2). *Pois, pronto,
   então, olha, epá, se calhar, á vontade.* These carry no grammatical load and are the
   fastest route to sounding resident rather than touring. Ours sit 100+ units too late.
   → **Move/duplicate to B1.**

8. **Ordinal numbers** (they: A2.43). Floors of a building (*terceiro andar*), dates,
   *o primeiro / o segundo*. Checked: our A2.7 `medidas-e-quantidades` already covers the
   partitives and collectives well (*meio quilo, uma dúzia, uma fatia, um molho*), so the
   gap is narrower than theirs — it is ordinals and fractions specifically, not quantities.
   Cheapest fix is to extend A1.19 `bigger-numbers-and-prices` or A2.7 rather than add a
   whole unit. → **Extend an existing unit at A2.**

Below the line, worth doing but lower urgency:

9. **Body parts: bones and organs** (B1.17) — supports `no-centro-de-saude`, which currently
   asks learners to describe symptoms with only A2 surface anatomy. → B1.
10. **Religion, festas and family rituals** (B2.11) — batizados, funerais, romarias, Fátima.
    Socially important, linguistically contained. → B2.
11. **Justice, military and activism** (B2.16) — completes the news/current-affairs domain we
    start in B2.14/B2.15. → B2.
12. **Buying a house, part 2** (B2.6) — the professionals and the money: escritura, IMI,
    notário, mediador, crédito habitação. Our single B1.16 unit is doing the work of their
    two. → B2, or split B1.16.
13. **Explicit subject-pronoun unit** (A1.4) — ours is embedded in the present-tense units.
    Defensible, since EP drops subject pronouns heavily; low priority.
14. **The verb andar** (B2.2) — genuinely useful (*ando a aprender*, *como andas?*) but can
    be folded into the ficar unit or the auxiliaries unit rather than given its own.

## 3. What WE have that THEY lack

**Resident bureaucracy — the strongest differentiator.** PP covers Police, Disasters, Post
Office, Bank, Utilities, Renting and Driving. They have nothing on:

- `financas-e-o-nif` (B1.6) — NIF and the tax office
- `camara-municipal-e-residencia` (B1.7) — residency paperwork
- `conduzir-imt-e-carta` (B1.20) — exchanging a licence at the IMT
- `na-estrada-inspecao-e-gnr` (B1.21) — inspeção, portagens, GNR stops
- `escola-e-caderneta` (B1.22) — the caderneta and talking to teachers
- `no-centro-de-saude` (B1.15) — the health centre specifically, not just a hospital
- `chamar-um-canalizador` (B1.11) — calling a tradesperson
- `emergencias-e-o-112` (A2.36) — 112 specifically
- `registo-formal-e-burocracia` (B1.1) — the formal register itself, as a unit

PP is built for learners who visit Portugal. We are built for people who live there. This is
the part of the course no competitor will ever replicate, and it is worth more than the
eight gaps in section 2 combined.

**Local units, entirely ours.** `torres-vedras-market` (A1.27), `feira-de-torres-vedras`
(A2.12), `praia-e-surf-em-santa-cruz` (A2.40), `o-oeste-feira-vinho-carnaval` (B1.26),
`sotaque-do-oeste` (B1.28), `carnaval-de-torres` (B2.19), `as-linhas-de-torres-vedras`
(B2.22), `sotaques-e-falares-regionais` (B2.18).

**Trade counters.** `no-talho-e-na-peixaria` (A2.11). PP has Supermarket but not the talho
or peixaria — which is where the actual Portuguese interaction happens, and where the
register and the vocabulary are hardest.

**Contrast and consolidation units.** PP has none. Ours: `perfeito-ou-imperfeito` (A2.25),
`onde-por-o-pronome` (A2.33), `condicional-na-pratica` (B1.5),
`infinitivo-pessoal-com-preposicoes` (B1.14), `discurso-indireto-no-passado` (B2.17),
`infinitivo-pessoal-e-conjuntivo` (B2.21).

**The synthetic future as its own unit** (`futuro-do-indicativo`, A2.44). PP does not teach
the futuro simples in any dedicated unit across all 137.

**Register and pragmatics.** `humor-ironia-e-delicadeza` (B2.10) — softening, irony and
politeness moves. PP teaches slang and interjections but never how to be indirect, which is
most of Portuguese politeness.

**Pedagogical framing.** `pronuncia-europeia` (B1.2) is framed around vowel reduction — the
actual reason learners can't parse spoken EP. Theirs is a general pronunciation unit.

## 4. Level-placement disagreements

**The imperfeito, moved to sit right after the perfeito. We are right, decisively.**
We teach perfeito at A2.15/19/20, imperfeito at A2.24, and contrast at A2.25 — a five-unit
span. They teach Simple Past at A2.6 and Past Continuous at **A2.54**: forty-eight units
apart, at the very end of the level, with no contrast unit anywhere. Their sequencing
guarantees the learner spends most of A2 building a "past tense = perfeito" habit that then
has to be dismantled. Choosing between the two is the hard part for English speakers, not
conjugating either one, and a contrast is impossible to teach when the halves are a level
apart. Their placement looks like content-calendar residue — Past Continuous reads as a unit
that was added late and appended rather than inserted.

**The imperative moved early in A2. We are right, and the obvious objection is already
handled.** Ours at A2.4–5, theirs at A2.52. The imperative is mostly something you
*receive*: *diga*, *faça favor*, *siga em frente*, *tenha paciência*, *desculpe*. A learner
who reaches unit 51 without it cannot parse a counter interaction or a road sign, and cannot
speak to children. Early is right.

The objection to teaching it early is that the negative and courtesy forms are
morphologically the present subjunctive, which we do not teach until B2.7. Checked: A2.5
`imperativo-formal-e-negativo` already instructs that the negative forms (*não fales, não
comas*) and courtesy forms (*faça, diga, venha, vá*) be presented as fixed sets, with an
explicit "do not name the subjunctive at this level", and it anchors the whole unit on the
*fala!* / *não fales!* contrast. That is the correct handling and it removes the objection
entirely. Only follow-up: add a back-reference from B2.7 `presente-do-conjuntivo` so the
learner retrospectively sees that the forms memorised at A2 were the conjuntivo all along.

**The synthetic future given its own A2 unit. We are right, and they have a real hole.**
They never give the futuro simples a unit at all, teaching only ir + infinitive at A1.28.
Defensible for a tourist — spoken EP overwhelmingly prefers the periphrastic form. Not
defensible for a resident, who meets the synthetic future in signage, contracts, news and
formal correspondence constantly. It is also the morphological base of the conditional:
they teach the conditional at B1.5 with no synthetic-future foundation, which makes the
conditional look arbitrary. Ours at A2.44 sits immediately before B1.4 `modo-condicional` —
the correct order.

**Where THEY are right and we are wrong:**

- **Discourse markers / conversational fillers.** Theirs at B1.3, ours at B2.2. Ours is
  roughly 100 units too late for material that has near-zero grammatical prerequisite and
  the highest per-unit payoff in perceived fluency. Move to B1, or seed a light version at
  late A2.
- **Cognates at A2.8.** Placing the cognate-pattern unit early, before the heavy vocabulary
  levels, front-loads a multiplier that pays off across the rest of the course. We have no
  such unit at any level; if we add it, add it early rather than next to `falsos-amigos`.

**Where we agree, and both may be wrong:** both courses put the present subjunctive at B2
(theirs B2.3, ours B2.7). For a resident it arrives late — *espero que*, *talvez*, *é
melhor que* and the polite negative imperative all need it, and all are B1-level social
needs. Worth considering a light B1 introduction limited to high-frequency triggers, with
the full treatment left at B2.

**Where we agree and are both right:** tu/você early (ours A1.2 is better than their A1.8,
but both are early); ser/estar in the first third of A1; personal infinitive at B1;
compound tenses at B2. Our clitic placement at A2.33 versus their B1.15 favours us for EP
specifically — EP is enclitic by default with próclise triggers everywhere, and learners who
meet placement late produce Brazilian-sounding *me diz*.

## 5. Granularity — 137 to our 126, but their A2 is 55 to our 44

Almost all of their extra A2 mass is numbered sequels, and most of it is content-calendar
residue rather than pedagogy. A subscription product needs a steady drip of releases, and
splitting one vocabulary set into two yields two release slots.

Their numbered pairs and triples: -AR Verbs 1/2/3/4, -ER, -IR (six units for what we do in
two); Prepositions 1/2/3; Adverbs 1/2/3; Numbers 1/2/3; Adjectives 1/2/3; Places 1/2;
Things 1/2; Family 1/2; Body Parts 1/2; Professions 1/2; Animals 1/2; Simple Past 1/2/3;
Conjunctions 1/2; Clitic Pronouns 1/2; Buying a House 1/2; Compound Tenses 1/2; Idioms 1/2.

**Where the split is pedagogically real (3 cases):**
- *Conjunctions 1/2* — coordinating vs subordinating are different objects, and the
  subordinating set later triggers the conjuntivo. Our single `conjuncoes` (A2.37) is
  arguably under-built; consider splitting.
- *Clitic Pronouns 1/2* — 1st/2nd person clitics vs the merged 3rd-person forms (lho, lha)
  are a genuine difficulty step. We already split this correctly across A2.30, B1.18, B1.19.
- *Compound Tenses 1/2* — indicative vs subjunctive. We already split this (B2.12, B2.13).

**Where the split is padding:** everything else. Six units of present-tense verb drilling is
the clearest case — the second, third and fourth -AR units teach no new rule, only more
verbs. That is spaced practice, which belongs in the review scheduler, not in the syllabus.
We already have FSRS doing that job properly, so replicating their unit count here would be
strictly worse for us.

**The one real argument for their approach:** finer units are shorter units, and short units
suit a fifteen-minute daily habit better than long ones. Their 137 units are individually
lighter than our 126. If our units are running long, the fix is to split *within* our
existing structure at natural seams — not to import their numbering.

**Where they are coarser than us:** the entire resident-bureaucracy domain, and consolidation
units, of which they have none.

**Net verdict:** do not chase 137. The gap between 126 and 137 is not a coverage gap; the
real coverage gaps are the twelve units in section 2, and adding those brings us to ~136 with
materially better content than theirs.
