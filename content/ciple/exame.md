# CIPLE — Certificado Inicial de Português Língua Estrangeira (A2)

> Master dossier on the A2 exam that immigrants in Portugal need for nationality: what it contains, how it is scored, what it costs, when it runs, why seats are scarce, and how to prepare in 12 weeks. Verified against CAPLE's own site and the Diário da República, August 2026.

**Status of this document:** researched 9–10 August 2026. Everything below is sourced in `## Fontes`. Where a fact could not be verified from an official source it is marked **NÃO VERIFICADO** with a note on what is missing. Nothing here is invented.

---

## 1. What the CIPLE is

- Full name: **Certificado Inicial de Português Língua Estrangeira**.
- Level: **A2** of the Common European Framework (CEFR / QECR).
- Awarded by **CAPLE** — Centro de Avaliação e Certificação de Português Língua Estrangeira, a unit of the **Faculdade de Letras da Universidade de Lisboa**. CAPLE is a member of ALTE (the ALTE logo appears on its site).
- It sits inside a ladder of CAPLE exams: **ACESSO** (A1), **CIPLE** (A2), **DEPLE** (B1), **DIPLE** (B2), **DAPLE** (C1), **DUPLE** (C2), plus **TEJO** (a certificate for children).
- CAPLE's own FAQ states plainly: *"the CIPLE exam (level A2) is the minimum level for acquiring Portuguese nationality"* — i.e. this is the exam the nationality process is built around.

---

## 2. Structure: components, duration, weight

Three components, sat as one exam. Total contact time is roughly **2 hours** plus the oral slot.

| # | Component (exact CAPLE name) | Duration | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Compreensão da Leitura e Produção e Interação Escritas** | 1h15 | **45%** |
| 2 | **Compreensão do Oral** | 30 min | **30%** |
| 3 | **Produção e Interação Orais** | 15 min **per pair of candidates** | **25%** |

### What each component actually contains

**Compreensão da Leitura e Produção e Interação Escritas** (CAPLE's wording, verbatim):
- *"Parte I: Leitura de avisos colocados em lugares públicos e de pequenos textos. São usados itens de escolha múltipla e de associação simples."*
- *"Parte II: Produção de dois pequenos textos sobre tópicos adequados ao nível."*

**Compreensão do Oral:**
- *"Audição de textos, de registo informal"*, drawn from public, professional or educational contexts, tested with multiple-choice items.

**Produção e Interação Orais** (verbatim):
- *"Parte I: Interacção entre o examinador e os candidatos sobre identificação pessoal."*
- *"Parte II: Simulação de situações de comunicação do domínio público ou pessoal."*
- *"Parte III: Interacção entre o examinador e os candidatos sobre um tópico relativo a acontecimentos da actualidade."*

Note the oral is run **in pairs** — you are examined alongside another candidate, and Part III is a short discussion of a current-affairs topic. CAPLE's registration form requires candidates to consent to **video recording of the oral component**.

> **Nota:** the exam *page* does not publish items-per-part or word counts, but CAPLE's own model papers (linked from it) do: 20 reading items in 4 tasks, 25 listening questions, and written texts of 25–35 and c. 60–80 words — see leitura-escrita.md and compreensao-oral.md. (For calibration only: the 2006 ministry *teste de diagnóstico* — a different instrument — asked for a text of 50–70 words. Do not present that as the CIPLE spec.)

## 3. Scoring and the pass rule

CAPLE publishes three pass bands (verbatim):

> *"A classificação de Muito Bom é atribuída aos candidatos que obtiverem entre 85% e 100%. A classificação de Bom é atribuída aos candidatos que obtiverem entre 70% e 84%. A classificação de Suficiente é atribuída aos candidatos que obtiverem entre 55% e 69%."*

| Band | Score |
|---|---|
| **Muito Bom** | 85%–100% |
| **Bom** | 70%–84% |
| **Suficiente** | 55%–69% |
| *(no certificate)* | below 55% |

**The pass mark is 55% overall**, computed as the weighted sum: 0.45 × reading/writing + 0.30 × listening + 0.25 × speaking.

> **NÃO VERIFICADO — this matters for strategy:** CAPLE does not publish a **minimum mark per component**. On the published information, a candidate could in principle compensate a weak component with a strong one. Do **not** teach "you can fail one paper and still pass" as fact until CAPLE confirms it — ask CAPLE or a LAPE directly. The label below 55% is **Insuficiente** — published in the CAPLE FAQ («Insuficiente, Suficiente, Bom ou Muito Bom»).

There is no published expiry date for the certificate — **NÃO VERIFICADO**; CAPLE's FAQ is silent on validity.

## 4. The school version (CIPLE-e / "versão escolar")

Yes, it exists. CAPLE, verbatim:

> *"Este exame tem uma versão escolar destinada a jovens com idades compreendidas entre os 12 e os 15 anos. Nesta versão, as tarefas dos exames estão adaptadas à idade dos candidatos."*

- Listed on CAPLE's exam index as **CIPLE-e**, alongside ACESSO-e, DEPLE-e and DIPLE-e.
- Age ranges per the FAQ: **standard exams from 16 years old**; **adapted (school) versions for ages 12–15** at A2/B1/B2; **TEJO for ages 9–11**.
- Tension to flag: the online registration flow asks the candidate to confirm they are **18+ or have parental authorisation**. So under-18s can register with parental authorisation, but the *school* version is the age-appropriate paper for 12–15. Worth confirming with the chosen LAPE for a 16–17 year-old.

## 5. Who actually needs the CIPLE

### 5.1 Citizenship (naturalisation) — the law changed on 19 May 2026

**Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, de 18 de maio** amended the Lei da Nacionalidade (Lei n.º 37/81). It was approved 1 April 2026, promulgated 3 May 2026 by President António José Martins Seguro, referenda 4 May 2026 by PM Luís Montenegro, published 18 May 2026, and **entered into force the day after publication — 19 May 2026** (Art. 8.º). A *Declaração de Retificação n.º 17/2026/1*, also of 18 May 2026, took effect 19 May 2026.

The naturalisation requirements now read (Art. 6.º, n.º 1, verbatim):

- **b)** *"Residirem legalmente no território português há pelo menos sete anos, no caso de nacionais de países de língua oficial portuguesa e de cidadãos de Estados-Membros da União Europeia, ou 10 anos, no caso de nacionais de outros países;"*
- **c)** *"Comprovarem, através de teste ou de certificado, conhecer suficientemente a língua e a cultura portuguesas, a história e os símbolos nacionais;"*
- **d)** *"Conhecerem suficientemente os direitos e deveres fundamentais inerentes à nacionalidade portuguesa e a organização política do Estado português;"*
- **e)** solemn declaration of adherence to the fundamental principles of the democratic rule of law;
- **i)** *"Possuírem capacidade para assegurar a sua subsistência."*

So alínea c) now bundles **four things**: language, culture, history, national symbols. Alínea d) adds **rights, duties and political organisation** — the civics component.

### 5.2 The CPLP exemption — read the exact scope

Art. 6.º, n.º 10, verbatim:

> *"Presume-se que os nacionais de países de língua oficial portuguesa preenchem o requisito da **primeira parte** da alínea c) do n.º 1, salvo nos casos em que a falta de domínio da língua portuguesa, evidenciada junto dos serviços competentes, seja manifesta."*

Three things follow, and they are the commercially important ones:

1. The presumption covers only the **first part of c)** — the **language**. It does **not** cover culture, history and national symbols, and it does **not** touch alínea d) at all. **CPLP nationals are exempt from the language proof but not from the civics/culture requirement.**
2. The presumption is **rebuttable** — it falls away where a manifest lack of command of Portuguese is evidenced before the competent services.
3. It attaches to being a **national of a Portuguese-official-language country**, as drafted here.

### 5.3 Residency

**NÃO VERIFICADO.** A basic-Portuguese requirement for permanent residence has historically existed in the immigration law (Lei n.º 23/2007), and the Government has announced a tightening of *"leis de nacionalidade e residência"*. I could **not** verify the current residency wording from an official source: **aima.gov.pt failed TLS verification** on every attempt from this environment, and the Diário da República consolidated-law pages render client-side and returned empty content to the fetcher. **Do not publish a residency claim until someone opens aima.gov.pt in a browser and reads the current requirements.**

### 5.4 The legal anchor for "A2"

**Portaria n.º 1403-A/2006, de 15 de Dezembro**, Art. 1.º, n.º 2, verbatim:

> *"Considera-se conhecimento suficiente em língua portuguesa o nível A2 do quadro europeu comum de referência para as línguas."*

That is why A2 — and therefore CIPLE — is the bar.

## 6. Alternatives to sitting the CIPLE

The Regulamento da Nacionalidade Portuguesa (**Decreto-Lei n.º 237-A/2006**, amended by **Decreto-Lei n.º 26/2022**) recognises more than one route to proving language knowledge, in substance:

- a school certificate showing Portuguese was completed for at least two academic years at a recognised institution;
- a certificate from a Portuguese-language exam run within public education, or accredited by the Instituto Camões when taken abroad;
- a **certificate in Portuguese as a foreign language from an evaluation centre** — this is the CAPLE/CIPLE route.

There is also the ministry **teste de diagnóstico** created by Portaria 1403-A/2006: fee **€15** in Portugal / **€20** abroad, pass mark **50%**, *Aprovado / Não aprovado*, results within **15 days**, run quarterly, with separate models for ages 10–14 and over-14. **Caveat: this is a 2006 instrument** and the surrounding regime has been amended since (DL 26/2022, and now Lei Orgânica 1/2026). **NÃO VERIFICADO** whether it is still being applied in 2026 and through which body — the old registration address given in the Portaria was `provalinguaportuguesa.gov.pt`. Verify before advertising it as a cheaper alternative, because if it still runs it is roughly **six times cheaper** than CIPLE.

### The regulation gap you must disclose to buyers

Art. 4.º of Lei Orgânica 1/2026: *"O Governo procede às necessárias alterações ao Regulamento da Nacionalidade Portuguesa, aprovado em anexo ao Decreto-Lei n.º 237-A/2006, de 14 de dezembro, no prazo de 90 dias a contar da publicação da presente lei."*

90 days from 18 May 2026 lands on **≈16 August 2026**. As of **10 August 2026** I could not confirm from an official source that the amended Regulamento — and therefore the operational rules and format of the new culture/history/civics **teste** — has been published. Secondary legal commentary states the language-test regulation had not yet been published and that no test was yet in application. **NÃO VERIFICADO in an official source.** Re-check `diariodarepublica.pt` after mid-August 2026 before making any product claim about the civics test format.

Also worth knowing: Art. 7.º, n.º 2 — procedures **already pending** when the law entered into force continue under the **previous** wording of Lei 37/81.

## 7. Registration: process, payment, fee

Registration is done online at CAPLE, in five steps:

1. choose the exam and the country;
2. choose the **exam centre (LAPE)**;
3. choose the **session**;
4. fill in personal data;
5. pay.

**Data required:** name, date of birth, gender, ID document type and number, **NIF**, address, postcode, city, country, nationality, contacts. Plus a mandatory statistical block: native language, two foreign languages, how long you have studied Portuguese, your motivation for the exam, education level, occupation, professional area, and the contexts in which you speak Portuguese.

**Payment:**
- Credit card with **3D Secure**, or — for candidates resident in Portugal — **referência Multibanco** or **MB WAY**.
- You have **24 hours** to complete payment after registering.
- If you do not pay, you must wait **48 hours** before registering again — during which your seat is gone.
- The fee is **non-refundable**.

**Declarations at registration:** acceptance of the exam regulations; consent to **video recording of the oral**; confirmation of being 18+ or holding parental authorisation. Candidates who are nationals/residents of Portuguese-speaking countries must declare their linguistic background so eligibility as a *foreign-language* learner can be confirmed.

### Fee (2026)

**CIPLE: €95.** Confirmed on two independent LAPE pages for the 2026 sessions (Instituto Politécnico de Leiria; ESS Cruz Vermelha Portuguesa – Alto Tâmega, Chaves), which also list DEPLE €115, DIPLE €135, DAPLE €155, DUPLE €170.

**Discrepancy to flag:** the Instituto Politécnico de Viseu (ESEV) LAPE page still shows an older table — ACESSO €42, **CIPLE €72**, DEPLE €92, DIPLE €112, DAPLE €127, DUPLE €142. **CAPLE does not publish a public price table**; the price is shown inside the registration flow. Treat **€95** as the 2026 figure and tell users to confirm the amount on screen before paying, because individual LAPE pages go stale.

## 10. The seat-scarcity problem (documented)

This is real, and it is the strongest reason a prep product exists.

- **Each centre sets its own capacity.** CAPLE, verbatim: *"cada LAPE decide o nível de exame que quer realizar e o número de vagas por exame"* — and the FAQ adds that centres decide *"how many vacancies open in each period, according to available resources and spaces"*, with only available slots appearing during registration. There is no national seat pool and no waiting list.
- **Seats sell out.** February and March 2026 CIPLE registrations at FLUL were reported exhausted; the April 2026 window opened 2 March 2026 at 15h00.
- **Candidates leave the country to sit it.** *Público*, 1 November 2025, reported immigrants travelling to **Madrid** to take the Portuguese exam because places in Portugal were sold out, and that the centre had **increased places by 53%** while supply in Portugal remained insufficient. **Partial verification:** publico.pt returns **HTTP 403** to this fetcher, so I have the headline and search-surfaced summary but not the full article text. Have a human open it before quoting figures publicly.
- **A fraud market has appeared.** *Público*, 18 April 2026, reports immigrants paying for Portuguese courses for nationality purposes and the circulation of **fake diplomas**, with free courses carrying waiting lists of months. Also 403 — **verify before citing**.
- **Downstream congestion is worse than the exam itself.** Reporting in 2025–2026 puts pending nationality applications at the IRN in the hundreds of thousands with multi-year waits. Secondary sources only — **NÃO VERIFICADO** against IRN data.
- **CAPLE's own transparency is poor:** the public `/estatisticas` page is stale, with **2020** as the most recent year (3 513 exams: 1 766 in Portugal, 1 747 abroad). No published pass rates by level.

**Operational rule to teach:** register **on the day the window opens, at 15h00**, and have your NIF, ID number and card ready — you have 24 hours to pay and 48 hours of lockout if you miss it. Watch several LAPE simultaneously.

## 11. Exam-day rules

- **ID:** original **cartão de cidadão nacional, passaporte ou título de residência**. Bring the same document you registered with.
- **Arrive 20 minutes early.** More than **15 minutes** late for the reading/writing component and you are not admitted.
- **Allowed:** scratch paper supplied by the centre; **blue or black pen** for the written answers; **pencil and eraser** for the reading and listening components.
- **Forbidden:** mobile phones, laptops, personal devices, dictionaries, books, notebooks, correction fluid/tape.
- **No communication** of any kind during the exam.
- **Absence:** miss the whole exam and you may submit written justification within **5 working days** to transfer to the next session — allowed **once only**. An unjustified absence from **any** component annuls the whole exam.

## 12. Results, certificates, appeals

- Results are published on the CAPLE website; you look them up with your **candidate number** at `/classificacoes`.
- **Timeline in practice (FLUL 2026 table):** results roughly **7–8 weeks** after the exam; certificates at the centre roughly **4 weeks** after results. Worked example: exam 19 Sep 2026 → results 9 Nov 2026 → certificates 9 Dec 2026.
- **Collection:** CAPLE's rules say certificates/diplomas reach the exam centre *"cerca de 21 dias úteis, após a publicação dos resultados"*; the FAQ says candidates collect them at their LAPE **15 to 20 working days** after publication. Minor inconsistency in CAPLE's own documents — plan for ~4 weeks.
- **Review (reapreciação):** request verification of the final result within **5 working days** of publication. CAPLE replies within **25 working days** of receiving the request. The decision is **final, with no appeal**.

## 13. Estratégia — sequencing 12 weeks of preparation

The weighting is the strategy. Reading + writing is **45%**, listening **30%**, speaking **25%**. Pass is **55% overall**. That means the reading/writing paper alone can carry almost half the mark, and it is the most trainable component for an adult who already lives in Portugal.

**Week 0 — before you study anything: secure the seat.**
Pick your session working *backwards* from the date you need the certificate in hand (exam → +7–8 weeks results → +4 weeks certificate). Register the day the window opens, at 15h00, with NIF and card ready. Identify a **second and third LAPE** as backup. Only then start studying — a perfect candidate with no seat certifies nothing.

**Weeks 1–2 — diagnose and build the survival core.**
Sit a full timed mock to get a baseline per component. Lock down the A2 spine: present, *pretérito perfeito simples*, *pretérito imperfeito*, near future (*ir* + infinitive), and **estar a + infinitive** for the present continuous. Personal identification to fluency — name, age, nationality, where you live, family, work, routine — because that is literally Part I of the oral.

**Weeks 3–5 — reading, the biggest single block.**
Train the two Part I task types daily: **avisos** in public places (station, health centre, supermarket, câmara municipal, farmácia) and short texts, with multiple-choice and matching items. Build the vocabulary of the notice itself: *encerrado, avariado, proibido, obras, horário, atendimento, senha, prazo, aviso, marcação*. Practise scanning for the one word that decides the answer rather than reading for full comprehension.

**Weeks 4–7 — writing, overlapping with reading.**
Part II is **two short texts**. Drill a small set of formats — message to a friend, note explaining an absence, short email, form-style personal message — each with a memorised skeleton: greeting, reason, two details, closing. Teach *tu* for friends and *você/formal* for services. Rehearse the connectors that buy marks at A2: *porque, mas, depois, também, por isso, quando*. Write three short texts a week and correct them properly; volume beats length.

**Weeks 6–9 — listening.**
30% of the mark for 30 minutes of work per session. Feed **European Portuguese only** — RTP, Antena 1, TSF, Portuguese podcasts — because Brazilian audio trains the wrong vowel reduction and the wrong pronoun placement. Focus on numbers, times, prices, dates, directions, and telephone/announcement register. Practise answering multiple-choice on a single listen, then a second.

**Weeks 8–11 — speaking, and specifically the pair format.**
Rehearse the three parts explicitly: (I) personal identification Q&A; (II) role-played public/personal situations — buying a ticket, booking at the centro de saúde, returning something to a shop, asking directions; (III) a short opinion on something **da atualidade**. Practise with a partner, because you are examined in a pair and you must both talk and react. Build a bank of five current topics you can say three sentences about. Learn to buy time in Portuguese (*Deixa-me pensar…*, *Se calhar…*, *Não sei bem, mas acho que…*) rather than freezing in English.

**Week 12 — full mocks under exam conditions.**
Two complete timed simulations in the real component order, with a real pen and the real 1h15 / 30 min / 15 min clocks. Score them with the weighting. If the weighted total is under 55%, spend the last days on whichever component moves the total fastest — usually reading, then speaking, never grammar theory. Confirm the venue, print the *convocatória*, pack the **original** ID, plan to arrive **20 minutes early**.

**Two cross-cutting rules for the whole 12 weeks**

1. **European Portuguese only.** The exam is Portugal-based: *pequeno-almoço*, *autocarro*, *casa de banho*, *telemóvel*, *estar a falar* (never *estou falando*), *chamo-me* (never *me chamo*). Brazilian input actively costs marks in the oral.
2. **If the civics requirement applies to you, prepare it in parallel — not after.** Since 19 May 2026 nationality also requires culture, history, national symbols (Art. 6.º/1/c) and rights, duties and political organisation (Art. 6.º/1/d). CPLP nationals are presumed to meet only the *language* part and must still cover the rest. The format of that test was not yet confirmed as regulated at the time of writing — see §6.

## Prompt context

O CIPLE (Certificado Inicial de Português Língua Estrangeira) é o exame de nível A2 do CAPLE, da Universidade de Lisboa. Tem três componentes: Compreensão da Leitura e Produção e Interação Escritas (1h15, 45%), Compreensão do Oral (30 minutos, 30%) e Produção e Interação Orais (15 minutos por par de candidatos, 25%). Na primeira lês avisos e textos curtos, com escolha múltipla e associação, e escreves dois textos pequenos. Na oral falas sobre identificação pessoal, simulas situações do dia a dia e comentas um tema da atualidade. As classificações são Muito Bom (85%-100%), Bom (70%-84%) e Suficiente (55%-69%); abaixo de 55% não há certificado. Há uma versão escolar para jovens dos 12 aos 15 anos. Em 2026 a taxa ronda os 95 euros e as épocas nacionais são 14 de maio, 16 de julho e 5 de novembro; o centro da Faculdade de Letras abre datas mensais. Inscreves-te no site do CAPLE e tens 24 horas para pagar. Cada centro decide quantas vagas abre, por isso esgotam depressa: inscreve-te no primeiro dia, às 15h00. Levas o original do cartão de cidadão, passaporte ou título de residência e chegas 20 minutos antes. Os resultados saem cerca de sete semanas depois e o certificado umas quatro semanas mais tarde. A lei considera conhecimento suficiente o nível A2. Para a nacionalidade exige-se também conhecer a cultura, a história e os símbolos nacionais; quem é nacional de país de língua oficial portuguesa só está dispensado da língua.
