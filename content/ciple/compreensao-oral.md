# CIPLE — Compreensão do Oral

> Everything a candidate needs to know about the CIPLE listening paper: its shape, its clock, its
> scoring, and the European Portuguese sounds that make it hard.

**Status of this dossier.** Every structural claim below is taken from CAPLE's own pages and from
the official CIPLE model exam (question paper, answer key and audio), all retrieved **10 August
2026**. The timings in *The audio, measured* were measured directly from CAPLE's own MP3, not
estimated. Where CAPLE does not publish something, this file says **NÃO VERIFICADO** rather than
guessing. Family: read the *Gaps and open questions* section before you trust anything to a
student.

---

## Where this component sits in the exam

CIPLE — *Certificado Inicial de Português Língua Estrangeira* — is CAPLE's **A2** exam, run by the
Faculdade de Letras da Universidade de Lisboa. CAPLE describes its purpose as attesting
*"uma capacidade geral básica para interagir num número limitado de situações de comunicação
previsíveis do quotidiano"*.

Three components:

| # | Component | Duration | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compreensão da Leitura e Produção e Interação Escritas | 1 h 15 m | **45 %** |
| 2 | **Compreensão do Oral** | **30 m** | **30 %** |
| 3 | Produção e Interação Orais | 15 m per **pair** of candidates | 25 % |

Total ≈ 2 hours plus one 15-minute break. Fee in 2026: **95,00 €**.

**Order and clock on the day** (CAPLE publishes both schedules):

- *Épocas nacionais* (the CIPLE-P sessions held in Portugal): written 09.00, **listening 10.30**.
- *Épocas internacionais* (May, July, November): written 15.00, **listening 16.30**.
- Speaking: time set by the local exam centre (LAPE).

So the listening paper is always **immediately after the written paper and the break** — you sit
down to it already 90 minutes into the morning. That is a real factor: 30 minutes of concentrated
listening on a tired ear. Practice sessions should replicate it, not be done fresh.

**Grades.** CAPLE publishes only overall bands:

- **Muito Bom** 85–100 %
- **Bom** 70–84 %
- **Suficiente** 55–69 %

The pass mark is therefore **55 % overall**. CAPLE's exam sheet lists the classification scale as
"55 % – 100 %".

> **NÃO VERIFICADO:** whether there is any *minimum per component*. Nothing on the CIPLE page, the
> *Normas*, or the FAQ states one; the published rule is a single aggregate. The FAQ does say
> results show *"as percentagens parciais obtidas em cada atividade linguística"*, so partial
> percentages exist and are reported — but no threshold attaches to them in any published text.
> Treat "you can fail listening and still pass" as **unconfirmed**, and prepare as if you cannot.

> **Site inconsistency, flagged honestly:** the CIPLE page's summary box says *"Nº de componentes:
> 4"* while the body of the same page names three. Three is what the exam actually contains
> (the written paper bundles two skills, which is probably where the 4 comes from).

## What the listening component actually is

Straight from the front page of the official model paper:

> *"Esta componente tem a duração de 30 minutos. Esta componente tem 4 páginas e 25 questões."*

So: **30 minutes, 25 items, two parts, on four printed pages.**

CAPLE's own format description:

> *"Audição de textos, de registo informal, próprios de situações de comunicação dos domínios
> público, profissional ou educativo. São usados itens de escolha múltipla."*

Note what that sentence commits to and what it does not. It commits to **informal register** —
these are not news bulletins or lectures, they are people talking in shops, offices, streets and
stations. It says "escolha múltipla" for the component as a whole, but the model paper shows Part 2
is in fact a **matching** task, so read "multiple choice" loosely as "select from given options,
nothing to write".

### The instructions you will actually hear and read

Reproduced from the model paper, because a candidate who has read these once will not waste
attention decoding them on the day:

> *"Vai ouvir os textos duas vezes."*
> *"Entre a primeira e a segunda audição de cada texto há um sinal sonoro."*
> *"Nos textos 1 a 4, tem 1 minuto para ler as questões antes de ouvir o texto."*
> *"Pode usar o enunciado para escrever as respostas."*
> *"No fim da audição dos textos, tem cinco minutos para passar as respostas para a folha de
> respostas."*
> *"A audição dos textos não pode ser interrompida e termina com a palavra FIM."*
> *"No caso de ter alguma dúvida, deve colocá-la agora."*

Three things follow, and they are the whole strategy in miniature:

1. **You hear everything twice.** Not once. The first listening is for gist and for locking onto
   the question; the second is for confirming the detail that decides between two options.
2. **The tape does not stop.** There is no "raise your hand". If you lose a text you must let it
   go and be ready for the next one, because it starts whether you are ready or not.
3. **Your last chance to ask anything is before it starts.** After that you are alone with it.

## Parte 1 — ten short texts, fifteen items (Questões 1–15)

Three options each (**A / B / C**), one correct. The number of questions per text is **not
uniform** — some texts carry one question, some carry two, and the paper tells you which:

| Texto | Questões | What it is, in the model |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | an interview — what the person can and cannot eat |
| 2 | 2 | a comment on the link between qualifications and getting work |
| 3 | 3 | directions to a *Centro* — floor and which door |
| 4 | 4 | two friends at the pool talking about sport |
| 5 | 5–6 | a customer in a shop: exchange/refund, and the shop's returns policy |
| 6 | 7–8 | a counter conversation about a **visto** and a **senha** |
| 7 | 9–10 | buying a bus ticket to Faro, and what to do with a 3-hour wait |
| 8 | 11–12 | starting a job application: forms, passport copy, competition for the job |
| 9 | 13 | street directions to the **correios** |
| 10 | 14–15 | living in the city centre vs outside it: rents, garages, tourists |

Read that list as a syllabus. Every one of those is a **transactional service encounter or a small
opinion exchange** — shop, counter, ticket office, street, poolside. That is exactly CAPLE's
published A2 can-do list for listening, which includes understanding *"questões/instruções de
rotina em postos de fronteira"*, *"instruções simples sobre orientação e deslocação no espaço"*,
*"informações relativas a preços de produtos e pagamentos"*, *"informações/instruções básicas em
correios e bancos"* and *"mensagens telefónicas simples"*.

**Where a one-question text differs from a two-question text.** A text with two questions almost
always splits into *what the customer wants* + *what the rule/answer is* (see Textos 5, 6, 8). Read
both questions before it starts; they run in the order the information appears.

## Parte 2 — ten sentences, ten places (Questões 16–25)

The task, verbatim:

> *"O que se diz onde. Vai ouvir 10 frases. No quadro abaixo, estão escritos os nomes de 10 espaços
> de comunicação. Faça a correspondência entre as frases que vai ouvir e os espaços de comunicação
> onde as frases podem ser ditas/ouvidas. Escreva as letras (A-J) na folha de respostas."*

The ten *espaços de comunicação* in the model paper:

**A.** escola · **B.** táxi · **C.** mercado · **D.** centro de saúde · **E.** agência de emprego ·
**F.** consulado/embaixada · **G.** restaurante · **H.** correios · **I.** aeroporto ·
**J.** loja de produtos informáticos

**The single most useful fact about this part:** in the official key the ten answers are
`H, D, C, B, F, E, J, G, A, I` — **every letter A–J is used exactly once**. It is a one-to-one
matching, not a "may be used more than once" grid. That changes the tactics completely:

- Answer the obvious ones first and **cross the letter out**.
- Two sentences fighting over the same place means one of them is wrong — go back.
- The last one or two can be got by elimination alone even if you barely heard them.

**Caveat, stated honestly:** CAPLE does not print "each option is used once" in the rubric. It is
true of the published model. Treat elimination as a strong working assumption and a checking tool,
**not** as licence to stop listening.

## The audio, measured

CAPLE publishes the model listening audio as a single MP3
(`caple.letras.ulisboa.pt/audio/CIPLE_CO_EXEMPLO.mp3`, 128 kbps, 28.8 MB). Its duration is
**1799.99 seconds — exactly 30 minutes**. That is the finding that matters most: **the audio *is*
the component.** There is no separate reading time before it, no extra time after it. Press play
and the 30 minutes are spoken for.

Structure, measured by silence detection on CAPLE's own file (threshold −40 dB, minimum 1.2 s):

| Clock | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0:00 – 1:11 | spoken instructions |
| 1:11 – 1:20 | pause to read the Parte 1 rubric and Questão 1 |
| 1:20 – 19:24 | **Parte 1** — Textos 1 to 10, each played twice |
| 19:24 – 19:39 | pause to finish the last Parte 1 answers |
| 19:39 – 20:04 | Parte 2 rubric read aloud |
| 20:04 – 23:59 | **Parte 2** — 10 frases, each played twice, roughly one every 23 s |
| 24:16 – 24:24 | *"FIM DA AUDIÇÃO DOS TEXTOS…"* |
| 24:25 – 30:00 | **silence — about 5½ minutes to transfer answers** |

The repeating cycle for each Parte 1 text, measured:

```
[ text, 1st audition:  19–57 s ]
   ~6 s pause
   ~2 s  sinal sonoro (the beep)
   ~4 s pause
[ text, 2nd audition:  19–57 s ]
   ~14 s  to finish answering
   ~1.5 s  spoken announcement of the next text
   ~9 s  to read the next question(s)
[ next text starts ]
```

Individual text lengths in the model, first audition: 34 s, 36 s, 37 s, 30 s, 32 s, 46 s, 37 s,
45 s, 19 s, 57 s. The two-question texts are the long ones; Texto 9, with a single question, is the
19-second one. Parte 2 sentences run **4–9 seconds**
each, also doubled, with a short beep between and about 5 seconds after.

### The uncomfortable implication

You get roughly **nine seconds** to read the questions before a text begins and roughly **fourteen
seconds** to finish answering after it ends. That is the real constraint of this paper. It is not
that the Portuguese is hard — at A2 it is not — it is that **there is no thinking time**. Anyone
who reads the question *after* the audio starts has already lost that item.

Trainable consequence: **pre-reading must be automatic**. During the ~9 s gap, do not read the
options word by word. Read the **stem** and the **first three words of each option** — that is
enough to know what to listen for (a time? a floor number? a preference? a rule?).

### Discrepancy, flagged

The printed model paper says *"Nos textos 1 a 4, tem 1 minuto para ler as questões antes de ouvir o
texto"*. **No 60-second gap exists anywhere in CAPLE's model audio** — the longest pre-text gap
measured is about 9 seconds, and there are ten texts in Parte 1, not four. The most likely
explanation is that the line is boilerplate carried over from a higher-level CAPLE paper (DEPLE /
DIPLE, whose Part 1 has fewer, longer texts). The model paper also still says *"Este CD não pode ser
interrompido"* on page 2 while page 1 says *"A audição dos textos não pode ser interrompida"*, which
suggests the PDF is a composite of two vintages and is **undated**.

**Practical ruling for the family:** prepare for the tight timing that the audio actually contains.
If the live paper is more generous, that is a gift, not a plan.

## Rules on the day that specifically bite the listening paper

From CAPLE's *Normas para a realização dos exames*:

- **Arrive 20 minutes early.** *"apresentar-se 20 minutos antes da hora marcada para o início do
  Exame"*.
- **Lateness is fatal here and only here.** For the written papers there is a 15-minute grace:
  *"chegarem com mais de 15 minutos de atraso às provas de Compreensão da Leitura e de Expressão
  Escrita não poderão realizar as mesmas"*. For listening there is **none**: *"não chegarem à hora
  marcada para o início da prova de Compreensão Oral não poderão realizar a mesma"*. One minute
  late and you do not sit it — and missing a component cancels the whole exam.
- **Pencil and eraser are allowed** for the reading and listening papers:
  *"os candidatos podem servir-se de lápis e borracha nas provas de Compreensão da Leitura e de
  Compreensão do Oral"*. (The written production paper must be in blue or black pen.)
- **Rough paper is provided by the centre**, and the model paper explicitly allows you to write on
  the question sheet itself.
- **No phones, no laptops, no electronic device of any kind, no dictionaries, no correction fluid.**
- **Do not write your name anywhere on the paper** — identifying yourself annuls the exam.
- **Return the answer sheet and the question paper** to the invigilator; not returning them annuls
  the exam.
- You must **stay in the room until the scheduled end**.
- Results are published on CAPLE's site; you may request a check within **5 working days**
  (fee 25 € in 2026). CAPLE does not release your answers or any per-item feedback:
  *"O CAPLE não dá informação detalhada sobre a qualidade dos desempenhos dos candidatos."*

**The answer-sheet trap.** You mark on the paper while listening, then transfer everything in the
final 5½ minutes. Two failure modes: (1) running out of time because you left all 25 to the end
without having decided them; (2) transposing — one skipped line shifts every answer after it.
Train the transfer as a separate drill: mark the paper cleanly (circle the letter, don't scrawl),
transfer in blocks of five, and read the question number on the answer sheet aloud in your head as
you go.

## What the marks are worth

25 items, 30 % of the final grade. **NÃO VERIFICADO:** CAPLE does not publish per-item weights.
*If* all 25 items are equal, each is worth **1.2 percentage points of the final grade** and
**4 percentage points of this component**, which puts the arithmetic in a useful light:

- To take 55 % of this component you need about **14 of 25**.
- To take 70 % you need about **18 of 25**.
- Parte 2 alone (10 items, elimination-friendly) is **40 % of the listening component** and about
  **12 % of the entire exam**. It is the cheapest part of the whole CIPLE to get right. Anyone
  drilling for time should drill Parte 2 first.

**Never leave a blank.** There is no published negative marking, and with three options a guess is
worth a third of a mark in expectation. In Parte 2, elimination makes a guess worth much more.

**Do not pattern-hunt.** The model key for Parte 1 is
`C C C B C B B A C C C B B C B` — one A, six Bs, eight Cs. That is one paper. Reading a "the
answer is usually C" superstition out of it is exactly how a candidate talks themselves out of a
correct answer.

## The pt-PT listening problem

This is why an otherwise competent A2 learner fails this paper. Portuguese as written and Portuguese
as spoken in Portugal are further apart than in almost any comparable language, and a learner whose
input has been Brazilian — which is most learners, because most media is — is effectively hearing a
different language.

### 1. Unstressed vowels reduce, and then disappear

In European Portuguese, reduction of unstressed vowels *"dá-se praticamente em qualquer sílaba
átona e em todos os dialetos"*: unstressed **a** → [ɐ], unstressed **e** → [ɨ], unstressed **o** →
[u]. And [ɨ] is frequently deleted outright. The word does not sound "reduced" to a learner — it
sounds **shorter than it is written**.

| Written | Roughly heard |
|---|---|
| telemóvel | *tlemóvel* |
| pequeno-almoço | *p'quen'almôç* |
| perguntar | *prguntar* |
| desculpe | *d'schculp* |
| está a chover | *tá-a-chvêr* |
| o que é que tu queres? | *q'é-q'tu qôrsh* |

Drill implication: **dictation is the single highest-yield exercise for this paper.** Listening and
"understanding" hides the gap; writing down what you heard exposes it in one line.

### 2. The "sh" ending, and what it does at word boundaries

Final and pre-consonantal `s`/`z`/`x` is **[ʃ]** — the English "sh". But it changes depending on
what follows, and this is where whole phrases become unrecognisable:

- before a pause or a voiceless consonant → **[ʃ]**: *as casas* → *ash cazash*
- before a voiced consonant → **[ʒ]** (the "s" in *measure*): *dois dias* → *doij dias*,
  *desde* → *dejd'*
- before a **vowel**, it links across the word gap as **[z]**: *os amigos* → *uzamigush*,
  *mais ou menos* → *maizoumenush*

The third one is the killer: **word boundaries vanish**. *Os alunos estão* is one long ribbon of
sound. A learner listening for separate words hears nothing.

### 3. `est-` collapses

*Estar*, *está*, *estão*, *estás*, *este*, *esta* lose their initial vowel in speech:
*estar* → *['ʃtaɾ]*, *está bem* → *tá bem*, *estás onde?* → *tásh onde*. Combine with rule 2 and
*"Está a estudar"* arrives as a single cluster. This is also the construction that Brazilian input
never trains: **European Portuguese uses *estar a* + infinitive, never the gerund**. *Estou a
falar*, not *estou falando*. *Ele está a trabalhar*, not *está trabalhando*. If you have only ever
heard the gerund, the pt-PT progressive does not register as a tense at all.

### 4. Dark L and final R

Syllable-final `l` is velarised and, for many speakers, close to a **[w]**: *Portugal*, *papel*,
*mil*, *hotel*, *Brasil*. Learners hear *Portugau*. Final `r` is weak. *Falar*, *comer*, *senhor*
lose their edge, so infinitives and nouns can be hard to tell apart at speed.

### 5. Nasals

*Não*, *mão*, *pão*, *irmão*, *tem*, *têm*, *bem*, *muito* (nasalised: *muinto*), *põe*, *mãe*.
Two consequences for the exam: (a) **negation is carried by a nasal diphthong that is easy to miss**
— *não pode* vs *pode* decides items like model Questão 1; (b) *tem* (3rd singular) and *têm* (3rd
plural) are near-identical in speech, so number is carried by context, not by the verb.

### 6. Clitics sit after the verb

*Chamo-me*, *dá-me*, *diga-me*, *sente-se*, *pode ajudar-me?*, *como se chama?* — and they move in
front after a negative or certain adverbs: *não me diga*, *já se foi embora*, *que se passa?* A
learner drilled on Brazilian *me chamo* will not parse *chamo-me* fast enough at speaking speed,
because the pronoun arrives where they expect the next word.

### 7. The words are different, and different words change the answer

The model paper is full of them. This is a **listening** problem, not a vocabulary problem: you can
know *autocarro* and still not catch it when a Brazilian-trained ear is waiting for *ônibus*.

| Portugal | Not (Brazil / elsewhere) |
|---|---|
| autocarro, comboio, elétrico, boleia | ônibus, trem, bonde, carona |
| pequeno-almoço | café da manhã |
| casa de banho, sanita | banheiro, vaso |
| telemóvel, ecrã | celular, tela |
| talão (de compra), fatura | nota fiscal, cupom |
| morada, apelido | endereço, sobrenome |
| ementa, empregado de mesa, sumo | cardápio, garçom, suco |
| frigorífico, rebuçado, fato | geladeira, bala, terno |
| senha (queue ticket), guiché/balcão | senha = password |
| multibanco, levantar dinheiro | caixa eletrônico, sacar |
| propina (tuition fee) | propina = bribe |
| talho, peixaria, papelaria | açougue, … |

Service-counter words that carry whole items in the model paper: **visto** (visa), **senha**
(the numbered ticket you take at a counter — Questão 8 turns on this), **impresso** (a form to fill
in), **candidatura**, **devolução/trocar**, **talão de compra**, **renda** (rent), **arrendar**.

### 8. Numbers, times and prices — the highest-value micro-skill

Model Questão 9 turns on **18h30 vs 14h30**. Model Questão 3 turns on **1.º andar, 2.ª porta à
direita** vs *à esquerda*. These items are decided by two seconds of audio.

- pt-PT teens: **dezasseis, dezassete, dezanove** (not *dezesseis, dezessete, dezenove*), and
  **catorze** (not *quatorze*).
- Clock: *às seis e meia*, *às seis e um quarto*, *um quarto para as sete*, *dezoito e trinta*,
  *meio-dia*, *meia-noite*, *da manhã / da tarde / da noite*. A 24-hour time can be read either
  way, so train both.
- *Quinze dias* means a fortnight. *Daqui a uma semana*. *Na semana seguinte à compra* — the
  returns rule in model Questão 6.
- Prices: *dois euros e cinquenta*, *vinte e cinco euros*, *quanto é?*, *quanto custa?*, *troco*.
- Ordinals and directions: *primeiro/segundo andar*, *rés-do-chão*, *à direita*, *à esquerda*, *em
  frente*, *ao lado de*, *ao fundo*, *no fim das escadas*, *vire / vira*, *siga / vai em frente*.

**Drill:** ten spoken numbers, times, prices and floor-plus-side directions a day, written down, no
replay. Five minutes. It is the cheapest mark-per-minute in the whole exam.

### 9. Discourse noise

Real informal speech is padded: *pronto*, *pois*, *ora bem*, *já agora*, *se faz favor* / *faz
favor*, *com certeza*, *não faz mal*, *está bem*, *olhe*, *então*. None of it carries the answer,
and all of it eats attention if it is unfamiliar. Hear enough of it that it becomes wallpaper.

## How the items are built to catch you

Verified against the model paper and key:

1. **Lexical echo.** The wrong option repeats words you definitely heard; the right one
   **paraphrases**. Model Questão 1: option A says *"é vegetariano e por isso não come nem peixe
   nem carne"* — vivid, quotable, and wrong; the key is B, *"não pode comer todo o tipo de
   alimentos"*, which uses none of the speaker's words. **If an option sounds like a transcript,
   suspect it.**
2. **The one-detail swap.** Questão 3: *1.º andar, 2.ª porta à direita* — right floor, wrong door,
   wrong side. Whenever an item involves a number plus a direction, expect all three combinations
   on the page.
3. **Number confusion.** Questão 9: *18h30* vs *14h30* vs *"o primeiro autocarro"*. Both times are
   spoken in the text.
4. **Negation and modality.** *não pode* / *tem de* / *precisa de* / *não é preciso*. Questão 11:
   *"apenas um impresso"* vs *"apenas o passaporte"* vs *both*. The word doing the work is
   **apenas**.
5. **Cause reversed.** Questão 4: *prefere nadar no mar quando está bom tempo* vs *nadar no mar é
   mais fácil por causa do bom tempo*. Same ingredients, inverted logic.
6. **Stance, not facts.** Questão 2 asks what the speaker's *comentário* was — an opinion item.
   *"ter mais e melhor preparação não quer dizer ter trabalho"* is a judgement, not a reported
   fact.

**Answer the question asked.** Several stems are about *what the speaker says/thinks* rather than
*what is true*. Read the stem verb.

## Preparation strategy

### The one exam-condition rehearsal you must do

CAPLE publishes the full model set. Do it properly, once, early — cold — to get a baseline, and
again late in preparation:

1. Print `CIPLE_CO.pdf` (4 pages).
2. Play `CIPLE_CO_EXEMPLO.mp3` **start to finish without pausing**. Thirty minutes, phone in
   another room, pencil only.
3. Mark on the paper as you go; use the final 5½ minutes of silence to transfer to a home-made
   answer grid numbered 1–25.
4. Mark it against `CIPLE_CHAVE.pdf` (its second half is Compreensão do Oral).
5. Score it out of 25 and convert: 14/25 ≈ the pass line for this component.

Do it after 75 minutes of written work, not fresh, at least once. That is the real condition.

> **Gap:** CAPLE does **not** publish a transcript of the model audio. So you cannot check *why*
> you missed an item, only *that* you did. Building your own transcript of the ten texts — by ear,
> slowly, with pauses, after the timed run — is one of the most valuable exercises available, and
> it is exactly the kind of thing this app can host.

### Weekly routine

- **Daily, 5 min — numbers dictation.** Times, prices, floors, dates, phone numbers. Written, no
  replay. See §8 above.
- **Daily, 10 min — pt-PT dictation.** 30–60 seconds of authentic audio, written out, then checked.
  Nothing else exposes vowel deletion so fast.
- **3×/week — one-listen discipline.** Take a 40-second clip; answer a comprehension question after
  **one** play. The exam gives two, so training on one builds margin.
- **2×/week — shadowing.** Repeat over the speaker at their speed. Producing the reductions makes
  you able to hear them; this is the mechanism, not a motivational claim.
- **Weekly — Parte 2 simulation.** Ten short overheard sentences, ten places, one-to-one matching.
  Cheap to generate, and it is 12 % of the whole exam.

### Input sources, all pt-PT

Portuguese radio and television are the correct diet, because the exam's speakers are Portuguese:
**RTP Play** and **Antena 1** (talk, news, phone-ins), **TSF** and **Rádio Renascença**,
telejornais on **RTP** and **SIC**. For the exam's actual register — informal transactional speech —
radio phone-ins and street interviews beat newsreaders, who are too clean.

**Do not train on Brazilian audio in the last two months.** It is excellent Portuguese and the wrong
Portuguese for this paper: different vowel system, no reduction, gerund progressive, proclitic
pronouns, different everyday nouns. Mixed input at A2 slows recognition of both.

### Vocabulary, organised by *espaço de comunicação*

Because Parte 2 is literally "which place is this said in", build vocabulary by **place**, not by
theme. For each of the model's ten places, learn the five phrases you would only hear there:

- **correios** — *selo, carta registada, encomenda, levantar, código postal*
- **centro de saúde** — *marcar uma consulta, receita, tenho dores, o número de utente, análises*
- **agência de emprego** — *candidatura, currículo, entrevista, contrato, a tempo inteiro*
- **consulado/embaixada** — *visto, autorização de residência, senha, impresso, passaporte*
- **aeroporto** — *embarque, bagagem de mão, porta, atraso, cartão de embarque*
- **táxi** — *leve-me a…, quanto é?, pode parar aqui, o troco, praça de táxis*
- **mercado** — *um quilo de, quanto é o quilo?, está fresco?, mais alguma coisa?, sacos*
- **restaurante** — *a ementa, o prato do dia, a conta, se faz favor, uma bica*
- **escola** — *a aula, o horário, o professor, os trabalhos de casa, a inscrição*

## Prompt context

A Compreensão do Oral do CIPLE (nível A2) dura 30 minutos, vale 30% da nota final e tem 25
questões em duas partes. Na Parte 1 ouves 10 textos curtos — conversas em lojas, balcões,
bilheteiras, ruas — e respondes a 15 questões de escolha múltipla com três opções (A, B, C); há uma
ou duas perguntas por texto. Na Parte 2 ouves 10 frases soltas e fazes a correspondência com 10
espaços de comunicação (escola, táxi, mercado, centro de saúde, agência de emprego,
consulado/embaixada, restaurante, correios, aeroporto, loja de informática); cada espaço usa-se uma
só vez, por isso podes eliminar. Ouves tudo duas vezes, com um sinal sonoro entre as duas audições.
Tens cerca de 9 segundos para ler as perguntas antes de cada texto e cerca de 14 segundos para
responder depois. No fim, tens cinco minutos para passar as respostas para a folha de respostas.
Podes escrever a lápis no enunciado. Se chegares atrasado a esta prova, já não a podes fazer.
Armadilhas do português europeu: as vogais átonas reduzem-se ou desaparecem (telemóvel soa
"tlemóvel"; está a chover soa "tá-a-chvêr"); o s final soa "ch" e liga-se como z antes de vogal (os
amigos soa "uzamigus"); diz-se estar a + infinitivo e nunca o gerúndio; os clíticos vêm depois do
verbo (chamo-me, diga-me); os números são catorze, dezasseis, dezassete, dezanove. Confunde-se
facilmente 14h30 com 18h30, ou 1.º andar com 2.ª porta à direita. As opções erradas repetem as
palavras que ouviste; a certa diz o mesmo por outras palavras.
