# Zona: Península de Setúbal

## Identity

The south bank of the Tejo is not a suburb of Lisbon. It is a place with its own history,
its own accent of self-description, and a long habit of being talked about by people who
have only ever crossed it on the way to the Algarve. Residents commute to Lisbon in huge
numbers and still do not call themselves *lisboetas*. They are from **Almada**, from
**Seixal**, from **o Barreiro**, from **Setúbal** — and collectively, from **a Margem Sul**
or, in the phrase everyone actually uses, **a outra banda**.

Administratively, the **Península de Setúbal** is a real unit, not a marketing label. The
NUTS III sub-region and the **Comunidade Intermunicipal da Península de Setúbal** — formally
instituted by Lei 75/2013 — cover nine municipalities: **Alcochete, Almada, Barreiro, Moita,
Montijo, Palmela, Seixal, Sesimbra and Setúbal**. Together they hold about **848 507
inhabitants** across **1 421 km²** (2024), a density of roughly 514 per km², with the city of
Setúbal as the administrative seat.

Three overlapping names cause genuine confusion, and Sandra should keep them straight:

- **Península de Setúbal** — the nine concelhos above. All nine are inside the **Área
  Metropolitana de Lisboa**, which has eighteen municipalities in total and splits into two
  halves: *Grande Lisboa* on the north bank and *Península de Setúbal* on the south.
- **Margem Sul** — narrower, and the term people use of themselves. It covers the six
  riverside concelhos that face Lisbon across the water: **Alcochete, Almada, Barreiro,
  Moita, Montijo and Seixal**. The name spread in the late 20th century, displacing the older
  **Outra Banda**, which survives in speech.
- **Distrito de Setúbal** — a different thing again, and the one that matters for paperwork.
  It has **thirteen** concelhos: the nine above **plus Alcácer do Sal, Grândola, Santiago do
  Cacém and Sines**, which are Alentejo Litoral and not on the peninsula at all. A form that
  asks for your *distrito* is not asking where you feel you are from.

The identity underneath all this is industrial and working-class, and only one generation
deep in the past. **Barreiro** was the **Companhia União Fabril** — CUF, founded 1865, driven
from 1898 by **Alfredo da Silva** into what was by the mid-1960s described as the largest
industrial group in the Iberian Peninsula and the fifth largest chemical conglomerate in
Europe. Its workforce went from about 2 000 in 1916 to some 20 000 by the late 1960s. It was
nationalised in 1975 and renamed **Quimigal** in 1977; the site trades today as the **Baía do
Tejo** business park.

**Almada** was **Lisnave**. The company was constituted on 11 September 1961; the
**Margueira** yard opened on 23 June 1967 and became the main facility. In 1969 Lisnave held
some 39 % of the world's ship-repair volume, and its **Doca 13**, opened in 1971, was the
largest dry dock in the world, taking vessels up to a million tons. The Margueira yard closed
after 2000 and the work moved to **Mitrena**, on the Setúbal side.

**Seixal** was cork — a century ago it was the country's principal cork centre, home to the
Mundet and Wicander firms, with the Fábrica da Amora glassworks alongside — and later steel,
with the **Siderurgia Nacional** inaugurated at Paio Pires in 1961.

Those factories pulled in people. **Baixa da Banheira**, in the concelho da Moita, filled in
the 1930s and 40s with families from the Algarve, Trás-os-Montes and the Alentejo looking for
work at the CP railway sheds, at CUF and at the steelworks. That is why the Margem Sul has
never sounded or voted or eaten quite like the north bank: it was assembled out of internal
migration, around a factory gate, within living memory.

And then the other half of the zone is not industrial at all. South and east of the built-up
strip the peninsula turns into the **Serra da Arrábida**, the wine and cheese country of
**Azeitão**, the fishing town of **Sesimbra**, the salt pans of **Alcochete**, and the Sado
estuary — where a resident population of bottlenose dolphins lives year-round, close enough
to the city that Setúbal's football club has adopted one as its mascot. A learner here can be
a commuter with a monthly pass, a fisherman's neighbour, or both.

## Geography & climate

The peninsula is a wedge between two estuaries: the **Tejo** to the north and east, the
**Sado** to the south. Its western edge is open Atlantic. That geometry explains almost
everything about daily life here, including the weather.

**The Serra da Arrábida** is the spine. The **Parque Natural da Arrábida** was created in
1976 (Decreto-Lei n.º 622/76, de 28 de julho) and covers about **10 800 ha** across Palmela,
Setúbal and Sesimbra. Its highest point is the **Pico do Formosinho** at **501 m**. The rock
is Miocene limestone lying over Jurassic limestone; the vegetation is Mediterranean
**maquis**, with *azinheira*, *sobreiro* and *carvalho*, and roughly **1 400 plant species** —
about 40 % of Portugal's flora on well under 1 % of its vegetated ground. The microclimate is
routinely compared to the Adriatic coast of Dalmatia.

Offshore, the **Parque Marinho Professor Luiz Saldanha**, created in 1998, protects about
**53 km²** of coast between **Praia da Figueirinha** and **Cabo Espichel**, holding more than
a thousand marine species.

**The two coasts are opposites, and residents choose between them by the wind.** The
Arrábida beaches — **Figueirinha, Galapos, Galapinhos, Creiro, Portinho da Arrábida** — face
*south*, into the Sado, sheltered behind the ridge. The water there is calm and unusually
clear; Portinho is a diving and snorkelling bay. The **Costa da Caparica** side faces *west*,
straight into the Atlantic, and gets the **nortada** — the north / north-westerly that gets
up in the afternoon from roughly June to September and, through upwelling, keeps the sea cold
all summer. So: a still, warm, glassy afternoon at Galapos and a wind-blown, cold-water,
board-shorts-and-a-fleece afternoon at Caparica can be the same Saturday. Deciding which
coast to drive to is a real conversation here.

**The Sado estuary.** The **Reserva Natural do Estuário do Sado** was created by Decreto-Lei
n.º 430/80, de 1 de outubro, covers **23 160 ha** across Setúbal, Alcácer do Sal, Grândola and
Palmela, is a Ramsar site, and is managed by the **ICNF**. Some **221 bird species** have been
recorded, including the **flamingo-comum**. The estuary developed in the shelter of the
**Tróia** spit — a **restinga arenosa** over 25 km long and 0.5–1.5 km wide, built up over
roughly the last 5 000 years.

**The dolphins.** A resident population of **roazes-corvineiros** (*Tursiops truncatus*) lives
in the Sado. It is the **only resident estuarine population in Portugal** and one of only
three known resident populations in Europe. You can see them from the Setúbal waterfront on a
good day. *(The size of the group was not confirmed in this research pass — do not state a
number.)*

**The fossil cliff.** Behind Costa da Caparica runs the **Arriba Fóssil**, protected as a
**Paisagem Protegida** since 1984 (Decreto-Lei 168/84, de 22 de maio), building on the 1971
**Mata Nacional dos Medos** reserve. It covers around **1 599 ha** across Almada and Sesimbra,
holds Miocene marine fossils — bivalves, gastropods, echinoderms — and includes the **Mata
dos Medos** and the **Pinhal da Aroeira**. Collecting fossils, plants or animals there is
prohibited.

**Cabo Espichel**, the south-western tip, is a bare, wind-scoured headland. Two natural
monuments — the **Pedra da Mua** and the **Lagosteiros** — hold **614 dinosaur footprints**
over about 1 350 m² between Boca do Chapim and Praia da Areia do Mastro, dated to around
129 million years ago (Lower Cretaceous).

**Erosion is a standing local worry.** The Caparica beaches have been visibly losing sand for
years; the responses are groynes and sea walls and periodic **reposição de areia**.

## Landmarks & buildings

- **Santuário Nacional de Cristo Rei**, Almada — the thing everyone sees first. Portuguese
  bishops meeting at Fátima on **20 April 1940** vowed to build it if the country were spared
  entry into the Second World War; it was inaugurated on **17 May 1959**, Pentecost Sunday,
  before some 300 000 people. The figure of Christ is **28 m**; the pedestal and portico bring
  it to **82 m**; the whole structure stands about **110 m** above the Alto do Pragal. Almada's
  own municipal description calls it the best viewpoint over Lisbon.
- **Ponte 25 de Abril** — six road lanes carrying around 150 000 vehicles a day, with a
  railway deck underneath opened on **29 July 1999** carrying roughly 157 trains and 80 000
  passengers daily. The toll (2,15 €) is charged **northbound only**. It is the single most
  discussed object in the zone.
- **Fragata D. Fernando II e Glória**, Cacilhas — built in Damão, Portuguese India, launched
  **22 October 1843**, in service 1845–1878, some 100 000 nautical miles sailed. The last
  fully sail-powered warship built for the Portuguese Navy and, by one count, the fourth
  oldest surviving warship frigate in the world. Restored 1992–97, shown at Expo 98, and
  moored permanently at Cacilhas since **1 March 2008** as a museum ship of the Museu de
  Marinha.
- **Castelo de Palmela** — donated to the **Ordem de Santiago** by D. Sancho I in **1186**
  (along with Almada and Alcácer do Sal); the Order's headquarters from before 1210 and
  definitively installed there from 1443. It stands at **240 m** on the eastern ridge of the
  peninsula, and on a clear day you see Lisbon from the keep. National Monument by decree of
  **23 June 1910**.
- **Forte de São Filipe**, Setúbal — D. Filipe I laid the first stone in person in **1582**,
  to a design by **Filippo Terzi**, with work continuing to about 1600 under **Leonardo
  Torriani**. Its chapel is lined with blue-and-white azulejos signed by **Policarpo de
  Oliveira Bernardes (1736)**. It was a *pousada* from 1964 until it **closed on 1 November
  2014**; since **31 March 2017** the Câmara Municipal de Setúbal has run it, with a bar and
  esplanade over the mouth of the Sado. Do not describe it as a hotel.
- **Convento de Jesus**, Setúbal — see *Churches*, but note the civic afterlife: it housed
  Setúbal's hospital until **1959** and reopened as the **Museu de Setúbal** in **1961**.
- **Mercado do Livramento**, Setúbal — inaugurated **31 July 1876**; the present building
  dates from **1930** and was remodelled in **2012**. Its **5 700 azulejos** from the 1930
  works, by **José António Jorge Pinto** and **Pedro Pinto**, show farming and fishing scenes;
  two further panels by **Rosa Rodrigues** were added at the north entrance in **1944**. It
  runs roughly 300 concessions. *USA Today* named it one of the world's most famous fish
  markets in 2015, and it stood in for a market in the 2014 telenovela *Mar Salgado*.
- **Moinho de Maré de Corroios**, Seixal — a tide mill built in **1403** by order of **D. Nuno
  Álvares Pereira**, later damaged in the 1755 earthquake, in private hands from 1836, bought
  by the Seixal council in **1980**, used for husking rice in the early 20th century,
  classified **Imóvel de Interesse Público** in **1984**, and now a nucleus of the Ecomuseu.
- **Arsenal do Alfeite** — the naval shipyard inside the **Base Naval de Lisboa** on the south
  bank. Construction started in **1928**, finished in December 1937, and it was inaugurated on
  **3 May 1939**; about 36 ha. It became **Arsenal do Alfeite, S.A.** on 1 September 2009.
  *(Whether the site falls administratively in Almada or Seixal was not confirmed — avoid
  claiming either.)*
- **Ruínas romanas de Tróia** — a very large Roman fish-salting complex, its **cetárias**
  arranged around a central patio, producing **garum** from the 1st to the 6th century AD,
  with baths excavated in 1956, houses, necropolises and the remains of a paleo-Christian
  basilica. National Monument since 1910. Note carefully: **Tróia is administratively in
  Grândola**, on the left bank of the Sado — reached from Setúbal but outside the nine
  concelhos.
- **Cacilhas** was doing the same thing 1 900 years earlier: Roman salting tanks operated
  there in the 1st century, also making garum for the empire.
- **Farol do Cabo Espichel** — the present tower dates from **1790** (a *farolim* existed from
  1430), stands 32 m tall with a focal height of 168 m, was automated in 1989, and opens to
  visitors on **Wednesdays, 14:00–17:00**.
- **Casa da Cerca**, Almada — a contemporary-art centre in the old town, open since **1993**.
- **Castelo de Almada** — the walls above Cacilhas; D. Sancho I took Almada in **1191**.
- **Universities.** Almada is described as the second largest university pole in the Área
  Metropolitana de Lisboa, with the **FCT NOVA** campus at Monte de Caparica, the **Instituto
  Piaget**, the **Instituto Universitário Egas Moniz** and the **Escola de Tecnologias
  Navais**. Setúbal has the **Instituto Politécnico de Setúbal**.

## Churches & religious sites

- **Igreja do Convento de Jesus**, Setúbal — the important one nationally. The convent was
  founded in **1489** by **Justa Rodrigues Pereira**, wet-nurse to the future D. Manuel I; from
  **1492** Queen D. Leonor intervened and made it the first house of the *Santa Coleta* reform
  in Portugal, with the first reformed nuns arriving from Gandía. The church is **Manueline**
  and is attributed to **Diogo Boitaca**; it has been a **National Monument since 1910** and
  carries the **European Heritage Label since 2011**.
- **Santuário Nacional de Cristo Rei**, Almada — a working sanctuary with a chapel and
  reliquaries as well as a viewpoint. Visiting hours: **October–March 10:00–18:00,
  April–September 10:00–19:00**, last admission 20 minutes before closing, at Alto do Pragal,
  Avenida Cristo Rei, 2800-058 Almada.
- **Santuário de Nossa Senhora do Cabo Espichel** — the most atmospheric religious site in the
  zone: a huge, half-empty pilgrim complex on a bare clifftop. A hermitage was raised in the
  mid-14th century for an image of the Virgin venerated on the rock. The **Igreja de Nossa
  Senhora do Cabo** was begun in **1701** and consecrated in **1707**; the long lodging ranges,
  the **Casa dos Círios**, were built in **1715**, with further pilgrim housing recorded on a
  plaque as built by the *círio saloio* in **1757**. A **círio** is an organised collective
  pilgrimage from a parish; the **Círio Saloio**, begun in **1430**, drew on thirty parishes
  north of Lisbon and paid for the main hospice. The little **Ermida da Memória** on the cliff
  edge holds 18th-century blue-and-white azulejo panels.
- **Convento da Arrábida** — founded **1538–39** by **Frei Martinho de Santa Maria**, an
  Andalusian barefoot friar, on land granted by **D. João de Lencastre, 1.º Duque de Aveiro**.
  Its friars became the **Franciscanos Descalços Alcantarinos**, with statutes shaped by São
  Pedro de Alcântara. It sits on the southern, seaward slope of the serra, in the concelho de
  Setúbal, inside the natural park. It is **no longer a religious community** — it belongs to
  the **Fundação Oriente** — and has been an *Imóvel de Interesse Público* since **1977**.
- **Convento dos Capuchos**, Caparica — founded **1558** by **Lourenço Pires de Távora** for
  strict-observance Franciscans of the *Província da Arrábida*; the order was suppressed in
  **1834**; the Almada council bought it in **1950** and restored it in **1952**. It is now a
  municipal cultural venue inside the Arriba Fóssil protected landscape, not a ruin.
- **Igreja de Santiago**, inside the Castelo de Palmela — Gothic, built under **D. João I**
  (1385–1433).
- **Senhor Jesus das Chagas**, Sesimbra — the town's devotion, from a crucifix said to have
  come ashore in **1534** and venerated by the fishing families since. Its feast on **4 May**
  is the municipal holiday.
- **Círio dos Marítimos**, Alcochete — an Easter *círio*, running from Holy Saturday through
  to the Tuesday.

## Food & drink

**Choco frito is the flag.** Cuttlefish — **choco** — cut up and fried **without breadcrumbs
and without batter**, served with **batatas fritas** and a salad. It is the dish visitors to
Setúbal ask for by name, and the dish locals have strong, cheerfully unresolvable opinions
about. It is not exclusive to the city: the **Moita** riverside restaurants list *choco frito*
among their own specialities too. Say `um choco frito` and you are ordering a plate, not a
mood.

**Queijo de Azeitão** — a **DOP since 1986**. Sheep's milk from the *bordaleira comum* breed,
set not with animal rennet but with **cardo**, the thistle, worked for about forty minutes at
around 32 °C. The cheeses are small cylinders, roughly **8 cm across and 5 cm high, 100–250 g**,
and the paste is soft to the point of running. In 2014 it was named among the fifty best
gastronomic products in the world and took a Great Taste Award.

Two honest notes Sandra must not get wrong. First, the standard description of how to serve it
is to **cut it in half and then into small slices** — the widely repeated "slice the top off
like a lid and eat it with a teaspoon" is how a great many households actually do it, but it
is *not* what the reference description says, so treat the lid-and-spoon version as a habit
rather than a rule. Second, and more surprising: **the cheese is not really made in Azeitão.**
The DOP area covers Setúbal, Palmela and Sesimbra, and the main producers are around **Quinta
do Anjo**, near Palmela. A learner who says "vou a Azeitão comprar queijo à queijaria" is
buying it there, not watching it made there.

**Moscatel de Setúbal** — a fortified **DOC**, 17–18 % alcohol, made mainly from **Moscatel de
Setúbal** (Moscatel Graúdo), with the rarer **Moscatel Roxo** giving the purple style; the
rules require at least 67 % of the must from those varieties. The region was demarcated in
**1907/1908**, centred on Azeitão and taking in Palmela, Setúbal and part of the freguesia of
Nossa Senhora do Castelo in Sesimbra. Colours run "from light topaz to burnt topaz". It is
drunk as an aperitif or after dinner, and it is the standard thing to take to someone's house.

**The table wine.** The whole zone is the **Região de vinhos da Península de Setúbal** (a
Vinho Regional), with **Palmela DOC** and **Setúbal DOC** inside it. The red grape that
defines it is **Castelão** — and here, and only here, it is called **Periquita**. Reds also
use Aragonez, Touriga Nacional, Tinta Amarela, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot; whites use
Arinto, Fernão-Pires, Moscatel de Alexandria, Roupeiro, Malvasia Fina and Chardonnay.
*(The story behind the name "Periquita" is repeated everywhere and was not verifiable here —
do not tell it as fact.)*

**Torta de Azeitão** — a rolled sponge with an egg-and-sugar filling, faintly lemony and
cinnamon-scented, sold in every café and every roadside stop in Azeitão and treated as one of
the region's emblems. *(No founding date or origin story was verifiable — say "tradicional",
not a year.)*

**Fish.** Setúbal is a fishing and fish-canning city; **sardinha em azeite** was its industry
until the trade collapsed from the 1970s. **Sesimbra** is the live fishing town, and the
species genuinely associated with it is the **peixe-espada-preto** — the black scabbardfish —
which is landed chiefly in Madeira but, on the mainland, principally at Sesimbra. Be careful
here, because Portuguese makes it easy to go wrong: **peixe-espada** is *not* **espadarte**.
Espadarte is swordfish and has no verified Sesimbra connection.

**The Tejo dishes.** The riverside towns — Moita, Barreiro, Alcochete, Montijo — have a
gastronomy explicitly tied to the estuary: **caldeirada à fragateiro**, **massinha da
caldeirada**, **massa de peixe**, **massinha de sapateira**, **ensopado de enguias** (eel
stew), arroz de marisco, and **sopa caramela**. This is boatman's food, not restaurant
invention.

**Inland, at Palmela**: rabbit cooked in the regional style, **cabrito assado**, and the
pastries **fogaças** and **suspiros**.

**What is not from here.** Do not import Alentejo or Lisbon dishes into this zone to fill a
gap. Setúbal has no *pastel* of its own to rival the torta de Azeitão, and inventing one
would be worse than saying so.

## Restaurants & institutions

A note first, because it matters: individual restaurants could not be verified as still
trading in this research pass, so **none are named below**. What follows is the set of
institutions that were verified, plus the rituals — and the rituals are the part Sandra
actually needs.

**The Mercado do Livramento, Setúbal.** The single most important everyday institution in the
zone. Around 300 concessions under a 1930 hall lined with azulejos of fishermen and farm
work. The ritual: you do not walk in and buy; you walk the fish aisle *twice*, once to look
and once to decide, and everyone claims to have "a minha peixeira" — my fishwoman — whom they
have used for years and who will tell them what is good today and quietly steer them away from
what is not. Prices are argued about affectionately and never seriously. Go late and you get
the end-of-morning deal; go early and you get the choice. It runs **Tuesday to Sunday and is
closed on Mondays** — the Monday closure is the thing visitors get wrong and residents never do.
*(The precise opening hours were not verified; give the days, not the times.)*

**The choco frito argument.** Every family in Setúbal has a place, and every family is certain
that the other families are wrong. The argument is conducted with total conviction and
absolutely no consequences, and it is one of the safest and warmest conversations a learner
can join: you are not expected to win it, only to have a position. The correct move is to name
a place, defend it for thirty seconds, and then concede that "cada um tem o seu".

**The adegas of Azeitão.** Two verified, long-standing houses sit within a short walk of each
other in **Vila Nogueira de Azeitão**:

- **José Maria da Fonseca**, founded **1834**, described as the oldest table-wine company in
  Portugal and still run by the same family into a seventh generation. Its historic
  **Periquita** cellar and **Casa Museu** are at Azeitão.
- **Bacalhôa Vinhos de Portugal**, trading since **1922**, which runs the **Bacalhôa Adega
  Museu** and the **Palácio da Bacalhôa** at Vila Nogueira de Azeitão and owns **Quinta da
  Bacalhôa** at Vila Fresca de Azeitão. Visits to both are booked in advance.

The ritual around these is the Sunday-drive-with-relatives one: the tasting, the arithmetic
about how many bottles will fit in the boot, and the obligatory stop for cheese and a torta on
the way home.

**Pousada Castelo de Palmela.** The convent buildings inside the castle were converted into a
pousada from **1945** and have been part of the Pousadas de Portugal network since the 1970s;
it still operates. Locals mostly go up for the view and the *feriado municipal*, not to stay.

**Forte de São Filipe, Setúbal.** Under municipal management since 31 March 2017, with a bar
and esplanade. This is where you take a visitor at the end of the afternoon to look down the
Sado towards Tróia.

**Festival de Almada.** Founded in **1984** by **Joaquim Benite** (1943–2012), organised each
year by the **Companhia de Teatro de Almada** with the Câmara Municipal de Almada, running
**4–18 July**. Its home venues are the **Teatro Municipal Joaquim Benite** and the **Escola
D. António da Costa**, whose grounds became the festival's emblematic space in 1992 — the big
stage and, just as importantly, the esplanade where everyone sits afterwards. It is one of the
genuinely nationally significant things that happens on this side of the river, and Almada
residents are quietly proud that Lisbon has to cross the water for it.

**Ecomuseu Municipal do Seixal**, open since **1992**, with several nuclei devoted to
industrial and riverine heritage, including the tide mill at Corroios. **Casa da Cerca** in
Almada, contemporary art since 1993. **Museu de Setúbal** in the Convento de Jesus since 1961.
The **Fragata D. Fernando II e Glória** at Cacilhas.

**Football.** **Vitória Futebol Clube** of Setúbal, founded **20 November 1910**, plays in
green and white at the **Estádio do Bonfim** (15 497). Its supporters are the **sadinos** —
and its mascot is called *Sadino* and is a **roaz-corvineiro**, the Sado dolphin. Three Taças
de Portugal (1964-65, 1966-67, 2004-05) and a Taça da Liga (2007-08). The club motto, delivered
without irony, is *"Vitória não é grande, é ENORME"*. In Barreiro, **FC Barreirense** carries
the same function for the same reasons.

**Alcochete** holds two institutions that everyone in the zone knows by name: the **Freeport**
outlet and **Sporting Clube de Portugal's** training complex.

## Everyday life & errands

This is the section that defines the zone. Life here is organised around **crossing the
river** — or deliberately not crossing it.

**The cacilheiro.** The word properly means the boats that cross the Tejo, named after the
Cacilhas run; the first regular steam service from Cacilhas to Lisbon began in **1836**, and
before the bridge opened in 1966 cacilheiros also ran from Seixal, Montijo, Barreiro, Trafaria
and Porto Brandão. The operator today is **TTSL — Transtejo Soflusa**, formed by the merger of
Transtejo and Soflusa in **2023**. The routes people use:

- **Cacilhas – Cais do Sodré** — the classic, on traditional cacilheiro-type boats.
- **Seixal – Cais do Sodré** — catamaran.
- **Montijo – Cais do Sodré** — catamaran.
- **Barreiro – Terreiro do Paço** — the ferry-boat, historically the Soflusa route.
- **Trafaria – Porto Brandão – (Belém / Algés side)** — the western crossing. *(The Lisbon-side
  terminal on this route has been changing; TTSL's own site lists Pedrouços/Algés. Do not
  state a fixed terminal without checking.)*

The ritual of the cacilheiro is the most characteristic thing in the zone: the walk down the
gangway, the scramble for the outside seats on a good day, the gulls, and the moment coming
back at night when the whole of Lisbon lifts into view on your left and everyone stops talking
for a second. People who have done it twice a day for thirty years still look.

**Fertagus.** The private concession running **Roma-Areeiro to Setúbal** across the **lower
deck of the 25 de Abril bridge**. Its south-bank stations, in order, are **Pragal, Corroios,
Foros de Amora, Fogueteiro, Coina, Penalva, Pinhal Novo, Venda do Alcaide, Palmela, Setúbal**.
For anyone in Almada, Seixal or Setúbal with a Lisbon job, Fertagus and the boat are the two
options and people are loyal to one or the other.

**Metro Sul do Tejo (MST)** — this is the name to use; it is what residents say. Formally the
**Metropolitano Sul do Tejo**, and the company that operates it is **MTS, Metro Transportes do
Sul**, whose initials you will see on the tickets and the trams without anyone ever saying them
out loud. A surface light-rail network in **Almada and Seixal**, three lines over nineteen
stops. The names that matter are the stops people give as destinations: **Cacilhas** (for the
boat), **Almada**, **Pragal** (interchange with Fertagus), **Universidade** (the FCT NOVA
campus at Monte de Caparica), **Corroios**,
**Laranjeiro**, **Santo Amaro** and **Casa do Povo**. **It does not reach Costa da Caparica** —
the second and third construction phases, which would carry it across the Seixal concelho and on
to the Moita (Baixa da Banheira) and Barreiro, have been **suspended since 2008**; a separate
extension of the line from Universidade to Trafaria, through Costa da Caparica, is projected for
2029. Getting this wrong is one of the easiest ways to sound like you have never lived here.

**Crossing to Tróia.** The other boat in this zone is the **Setúbal–Tróia** crossing, run by
**Atlantic Ferries** under a public-service concession, every day of the year. Two kinds of
vessel, and the difference is the whole planning decision: the **ferries** take cars, sailing
from the **Doca do Comércio** on Avenida Jaime Rebelo across to **Cais Sul** at Tróia in about
**25 minutes**; the **catamarãs** are foot passengers only, from **Rua Teotónio Banha** to the
**Marina de Tróia** in about **15 minutes**. Worth holding on to: the moment you step off, you
are in **Grândola**, in the Alentejo — not on the peninsula at all.

**The bridges.** The **25 de Abril** is tolled **northbound only** (2,15 €) and jams
predictably at peak. The **Vasco da Gama** lands at Alcochete and is tolled only in the
Montijo→Lisboa direction; its southern approach feeds the A2/A12 junction towards Setúbal.
The everyday calculation — which bridge, or the boat, or the train — is a normal breakfast-table
conversation.

**Roads.** The **A2** runs south from the 25 de Abril bridge. The **A33 / IC32** is the
peninsula's own cross-link, 37 km from **Coina to Montijo**, concessioned to Auto-Estradas do
Baixo Tejo, touching Costa da Caparica, Trafaria, Funchalinho, Charneca da Caparica, Palhais,
Belverde and Seixal — the road you take to avoid the A2 and the bridge entirely.

**Buses.** Since **1 June 2022** the metropolitan network is **Carris Metropolitana**, which
covers the whole peninsula in two areas: **Área 3 — Almada, Seixal, Sesimbra**, operated by
**TST (Transportes Sul do Tejo)**, the historic south-bank company; and **Área 4 — Alcochete,
Moita, Montijo, Palmela, Setúbal**, with connections to Barreiro. Older residents still say
`o TST` for the bus regardless of what is painted on it. *(The livery colour for this area was
not verifiable — do not describe it.)*

**The pass.** Everything runs on the **Navegante** card. The **Navegante Metropolitano** is
**40 €/month** and covers all eighteen municipalities of the Área Metropolitana de Lisboa
across boats, Fertagus, MST and buses; municipal passes are cheaper and vary by concelho.
Sorting out, topping up and complaining about the passe is a genuine monthly errand.

**Beach habits — and the summer road closure.** From **4 June to 15 September 2026** the
Câmara Municipal de Setúbal runs **Arrábida Sem Carros**: between **07:00 and 20:00**, driving
is prohibited on the eastern stretch (Travessa da Casa da Saúde to Praia da Figueirinha) and
conditioned by parking availability on the western stretch (cruzamento do Pinheiro to Praia de
Galapos), covering **Albarquel, Figueirinha, Portinho da Arrábida, Creiro, Galapos and
Galapinhos**. You park free at the **Terminal da Várzea**, the **Mercado de Azeitão** or the
**Alegro**, or pay 5 €/day at Creiro, and take one of the beach lines — **4470** Setúbal–Creiro
via Azeitão, **4471** Setúbal–Albarquel, **4474** Alegro–Figueirinha, and the free shuttle
**4477** between the Creiro car park and Galapos. Separately, at **Portinho da Arrábida**
itself, weekend access is restricted between 09:00 and 18:00. Planning a beach day here means
checking a bus timetable, not just the weather.

**Caparica** is the opposite habit: the everyday, all-summer, get-there-somehow beach, with a
run of named praias — **Nova Praia, Praia do Castelo, Praia da Sereia, Praia de São João, Praia
do CDS** — down to **Fonte da Telha**, which is shared between Almada and Sesimbra, sits in a
hollow of the **Mata Nacional dos Medos**, was founded by *xávega* net-fishing masters in the
late 19th century, and still runs nine monitored beach sections. Correct a common myth: the
**Transpraia** mini-train that used to run the length of the sand was pushed out of the town
centre in **2007** by the Polis works, moved its terminus to Praia Nova, and then **stopped in
2020** with the pandemic and **has not returned**. People still talk about it as though it might
come back, and it may — but it does not run now.

**Shops and markets.** The chains are everywhere — Continente, Pingo Doce, Lidl, Minipreço —
alongside the Alegro at Setúbal and the Almada Fórum. Setúbal has the Livramento. **Azeitão
holds its *mercado mensal* on the first Sunday of every month**, roughly 9h00–17h00, on the
market ground at Brejos de Azeitão — produce, plants, clothes, cakes and handicraft, and one of
the best-attended open-air markets in the region. The smaller towns each run a *mercado
municipal* and a periodic *feira*; *(their individual weekdays were not verifiable in this pass —
say "vou à praça" or "vou à feira" rather than naming a day you have not checked.)*

**Admin.** Council business goes to the **Câmara Municipal**; *atestados* and small
certificates to the **Junta de Freguesia**; cartão de cidadão, Finanças and Segurança Social
mean an appointment in the concelho seat. Health is the **centro de saúde** where you are
registered with a *médico de família*. Children attend an **agrupamento de escolas**, with
**ATL** afterwards for working parents — which, in a zone where a parent may be leaving on a
07:20 boat, matters more than usual.

**The commuter shape of the week.** Out early, across the water or the bridge, back in the
dark; Saturday is the market and the errands; Sunday is family lunch, the serra or the beach.
And the standing complaint, delivered without real bitterness, is the traffic on the bridge.

## Festivals & calendar

- **Festas em Honra do Senhor Jesus das Chagas**, Sesimbra — **4 May**, the *feriado municipal*,
  from the crucifix said to have washed ashore in **1534**. The town's own day, fishing
  families first.
- **Feira de Sant'Iago**, Setúbal — the big one. More than **400 years** old, held at the
  **Parque Sant'Iago** on Avenida António Sérgio across the **last week of July and the first
  week of August**, and described as the main festival of the Setúbal region and one of the
  largest fairs in southern Portugal.
- **Festa do Barrete Verde e das Salinas**, Alcochete — the **second weekend of August**, over
  seven days (7–13 August in 2026). The green cap of the *campino* and the salt pans, together:
  the one festival that most clearly says this side of the river is not Lisbon.
- **Festa das Vindimas**, Palmela — **August/September**, the wine harvest festival. *(The
  grape-treading and fireworks details everyone repeats were not verifiable here; give the
  month, not a programme.)*
- **Festas Populares de São Pedro**, Montijo — **29 June**, with *touradas* and *largadas*.
- **Moita** — the *feriado municipal* falls on the **Tuesday after the second Sunday of
  September**, tied to Nossa Senhora da Boa Viagem.
- **Barreiro** — *feriado municipal* on **28 June**, marking the town's elevation to city in
  1984.
- **Setúbal** — *feriado municipal* on **15 September**, the birthday of **Bocage**.
- **Círio dos Marítimos**, Alcochete — Easter, Holy Saturday through to the Tuesday.
- **Festival do Queijo, Pão e Vinho**, Quinta do Anjo (Palmela) — **April**.
- **Festival de Almada** — **4–18 July**, theatre across Almada and into Lisbon.
- **Santos populares** in June, as everywhere: **Santo António** (13), **São João** (24) and
  **São Pedro** (29) — the last carrying real weight in the fishing and river towns.
- **The working year**: the **vindima** in late summer; the salt season at Alcochete; and the
  summer beach regime from early June to mid-September, which is a calendar event here in its
  own right.

## Language notes

There is **no distinct south-bank dialect**, and Sandra should not invent an accent. The
peninsula falls inside **Estremenho**, the central-southern group, in its **lisboeta**
variety — the one usually treated as standard European Portuguese and sometimes compared to
British Received Pronunciation. Standard central-southern features apply: **/b/ and /v/ stay
distinct**, unstressed vowels reduce hard, and *ei* and *ou* close, so **peixe** leans towards
"pêxe" and **outro** towards "ôtro" — though in Lisbon and its surroundings *ei* is often
centralised rather than fully monophthongised.

What genuinely marks speech here is **lexicon and reference**, not phonetics: river words,
factory words, and a set of place-names people use as shorthand for a whole way of life. It is
also, historically, a **mixed** speech community — Baixa da Banheira and its neighbours were
built in the 1930s and 40s by families from the Algarve, Trás-os-Montes and the Alentejo, and
that mixture is part of why the Margem Sul has always sounded like its own thing rather than
like the north bank. **Miratejo**, in Corroios (Seixal), sits in the origin story of Portuguese
hip hop: RTP2's *Pop Off* filmed rappers freestyling there around 1992, two years before
*Rapública*, the compilation treated as the genre's founding record.

**Words worth knowing here**

- `a outra banda` — literally "the other side"; the older, still-used name for the south bank.
- `a Margem Sul` — the modern name for the six riverside concelhos.
- `o cacilheiro` — the boat across the Tejo. `apanhar o cacilheiro` — to catch it.
- `o barco` — what people actually say for the ferry in daily speech.
- `a ponte` — never "which bridge"; if you are in Almada it is the 25 de Abril.
- `passar para o outro lado` / `ir a Lisboa` — to cross the river. Nobody says "ir à cidade".
- `sadino` — from Setúbal (also `setubalense`); and the name of Vitória's dolphin mascot.
- `roaz-corvineiro` — the resident Sado bottlenose dolphin. The everyday word is `os golfinhos`.
- `o choco` — cuttlefish. `um choco frito` — the plate.
- `peixe-espada` — scabbardfish. **Not** `espadarte`, which is swordfish. Keep them apart.
- `o cardo` — the thistle used to set queijo de Azeitão instead of rennet.
- `a torta` — in Azeitão, always the torta de Azeitão.
- `o moscatel` — Moscatel de Setúbal; `um moscatel` is a glass after dinner.
- `Periquita` — the Castelão grape, but only under this name in this region.
- `a serra` — the Serra da Arrábida, and by extension a day out. `ir à serra`.
- `a nortada` — the north-westerly afternoon wind; blame it for the cold water at Caparica.
- `a arriba` — the cliff; `a arriba fóssil` behind Caparica is a named protected landscape.
- `a restinga` — the sand spit; Tróia is *a restinga*.
- `as salinas` — the salt pans at Alcochete and along the Sado.
- `o campino` — the mounted cattle herdsman of the Tejo plains; the `barrete verde` is his cap.
- `a xávega` — the beach-seine net fishing that founded Fonte da Telha.
- `a fragata`, `o varino`, `a falua` — the traditional Tejo cargo boats, still built to scale
  by retired boatmen at Gaio-Rosário and Sarilhos Pequenos.
- `caldeirada à fragateiro` — the boatman's fish stew.
- `o passe` — the Navegante card. `carregar o passe` — to top it up.
- `a CUF` — in Barreiro, always the factory, never a hospital or a company today.
- `o estaleiro` — the shipyard; in Almada it means the Lisnave at Margueira, past tense.
- `o círio` — an organised parish pilgrimage, as to Cabo Espichel.
- `feriado municipal` — the town's own public holiday; different in every concelho here.
- `uma bica` — an espresso (correct here; *cimbalino* is northern, do not use it).
- `um fino` / `uma imperial` — a small draught beer; *imperial* is the Lisbon-area word and is
  the one used on this side too.
- `Costa de Caparica` vs `Costa da Caparica` — the official freguesia name uses **de**; almost
  everyone says **da**. Both are heard; neither will be corrected.

## Towns

### Setúbal

The capital of the peninsula and of the district — about **123 500 people** in the concelho
(2021) across 230 km², with roughly 90 000 in the city itself, in five freguesias including
**Azeitão** and **Sado**. Chartered in **1249** under the Ordem de Santiago, it prospered
medievally, was knocked back by the 1755 earthquake, and rebuilt itself industrially after the
railway arrived in **1860** — sardine canning, cement, cellulose and paper, fertilisers,
shipbuilding and a working commercial port. Birthplace of the poet **Manuel Maria Barbosa du
Bocage** (1765–1805), whose birthday, **15 September**, is the municipal holiday.

- **Landmarks**: the **Igreja do Convento de Jesus** and the **Museu de Setúbal**; the **Forte
  de São Filipe** above the town; the **Mercado do Livramento**; the **Casa do Corpo Santo**;
  the **Forte de Albarquel** on the shore road towards the Arrábida.
- **Food**: `choco frito`, and the fish counter of the Livramento as a way of life. Moscatel
  and Azeitão cheese are twenty minutes away.
- **Everyday**: this is the errand town for the whole south and east of the peninsula — the
  hospital, Finanças, the Politécnico, the big shopping. The Fertagus terminates here. The
  dolphins are in the estuary in front of the city, and residents are matter-of-fact about
  them in a way visitors find hard to believe.

### Almada

The most populous concelho on the peninsula — **177 268 residents (2021)** in just **70 km²**,
in five freguesias, city since **1973**. Historically shipbuilding, textiles, milling and cork;
in living memory, the **Lisnave** yard at **Margueira**, which opened in 1967 and closed after
2000, and whose absence still shapes the town.

- **Landmarks**: **Cristo Rei** on the Alto do Pragal; the **Castelo de Almada**; the **Casa da
  Cerca**; and at **Cacilhas**, the **Fragata D. Fernando II e Glória**.
- **Food**: seafood at Cacilhas, on the restaurant street up from the ferry terminal, eaten
  facing the water with Lisbon on the other side of it. The ritual is to arrive by boat, eat
  slowly, and catch a later crossing than planned.
- **Everyday**: **Cacilhas** is the hinge of the whole zone — boats to Cais do Sodré, Carris
  Metropolitana buses and three MST stops in a few hundred metres. Almada is also the second
  largest university pole in the metropolitan area, with **FCT NOVA** at Monte de Caparica, so
  the town has a student population that has nothing to do with the shipyards. Each July the
  **Festival de Almada** turns the Escola D. António da Costa into the busiest place on the
  south bank.

### Costa da Caparica

A **cidade** and freguesia of Almada since **2004**, with about **14 000 permanent residents
(2021)** and a summer population many times that. Thirteen kilometres of Atlantic sand backed
by the **Arriba Fóssil** and the pine of the **Mata dos Medos**.

- **Landmarks**: the **Convento dos Capuchos** (1558) above the cliff; the fossil cliff itself;
  the named beaches running south — Nova Praia, Praia do Castelo, Praia da Sereia, Praia de São
  João, Praia do CDS — to **Fonte da Telha**.
- **Food**: grilled fish and *bifanas* on the beach front in season; out of season a handful of
  places carry the town.
- **Everyday**: this is Lisbon's beach as well as Almada's, and in August the road down is a
  car park. Erosion is the real conversation: groynes, sea walls, and lorries bringing sand
  back. Two corrections worth holding: the **Transpraia** mini-train has not run since **2020**,
  and the **MST does not come here** — the extension through Costa da Caparica to Trafaria is
  projected for 2029.

### Seixal

**175 023 residents (2024)** in six freguesias — Seixal, Amora, Arrentela, Corroios, Aldeia de
Paio Pires and Fernão Ferro. A hundred years ago it was the **country's main cork centre**,
with the Mundet and Wicander firms and the Fábrica da Amora glassworks; then heavy industry,
with the **Siderurgia Nacional** at Paio Pires from **1961**. The **Baía do Seixal** created a
whole vocabulary of trades — *pescadores, marinheiros, moleiros, calafates, carpinteiros de
machado*.

- **Landmarks**: the **Ecomuseu Municipal do Seixal**, public since **1992**, and its finest
  nucleus, the **Moinho de Maré de Corroios**, a tide mill of **1403** classified in 1984.
- **Food**: estuary fish and the same Tejo repertoire as Barreiro and Moita.
- **Everyday**: the catamaran from Seixal to Cais do Sodré, and Fertagus at **Corroios** and
  **Foros de Amora**, make this a commuter concelho with a strong local pull of its own.
  **Miratejo**, in Corroios, has a claim on the beginnings of Portuguese hip hop.

### Barreiro e Moita

Two river towns joined at the hip by the same industrial century. **Barreiro** — **78 359
residents (2021)**, four freguesias, *feriado municipal* on **28 June** — was **CUF**: founded
1865, expanded from 1898 under **Alfredo da Silva**, at its height the largest industrial group
in the Iberian Peninsula, nationalised in 1975, renamed Quimigal, and now the **Baía do Tejo**
business park. The railway reached it in **1861**. **Moita** — **66 262 residents (2021)**,
four freguesias — grew on coastal shipping as the crossing point between the south of the
country and Lisbon.

- **Landmarks**: the Baía do Tejo industrial estate itself, read as heritage; the **Rio Coina**
  entering the estuary; and at **Gaio-Rosário** and **Sarilhos Pequenos**, retired boatmen who
  still build **varinos, faluas and fragatas** to scale.
- **Food**: the estuary list — *caldeirada à fragateiro*, *massinha da caldeirada*, *ensopado de
  enguias*, *massa de peixe*, *massinha de sapateira*, *sopa caramela*, and choco frito on the
  Moita waterfront.
- **Everyday**: the **Barreiro–Terreiro do Paço** ferry is the town's front door. **Baixa da
  Banheira**, in Moita, is one of the densest places in the country and was built by internal
  migration in the 1930s and 40s — CP, CUF and the steelworks. Both towns keep a **praça de
  toiros**.

### Montijo e Alcochete

The eastern end, on the Tejo where it widens, changed permanently by the **Vasco da Gama**
bridge in 1998. **Montijo** — **55 689 residents (2021)**, five freguesias — was called
**Aldeia Galega do Ribatejo** until **6 June 1930**, and still feels half-Ribatejano: *touradas*
and *largadas*, and the **Praça de Touros Amadeu Augusto dos Santos** built in the 1950s to
replace an 1888 arena. **Base Aérea n.º 6** was established there on 12 June 1954. **Alcochete**
— **19 145 residents (2021)** across three freguesias — is the salt town, headquarters of the
**Reserva Natural do Estuário do Tejo**, with **salinas** where waterbirds nest, and the
birthplace of **D. Manuel I** in **1469**.

- **Landmarks**: the salt pans and the reserve; the Vasco da Gama bridge itself; **Freeport**
  and **Sporting**'s training complex at Alcochete.
- **Food**: estuary fish, and the whole *campino* repertoire that comes with cattle country.
- **Everyday**: Montijo has its own catamaran to **Cais do Sodré** from the Terminal Fluvial do
  Seixalinho, opened 2002. Both towns run on the **Festa do Barrete Verde e das Salinas**
  (Alcochete, second weekend of August) and **São Pedro** (Montijo, 29 June) as the year's two
  fixed points.

### Sesimbra

**52 394 residents (2021)**, a working fishing town in a south-facing bay with the Arrábida
behind it and **Cabo Espichel** to the west. Sheltered enough that the sea is a different
temperature from Caparica an hour away.

- **Landmarks**: the **Castelo de Sesimbra** on the ridge and the **Fortaleza de Santiago** on
  the front; the **Porto de Abrigo** with its fishing dock and marina; the **Santuário de Nossa
  Senhora do Cabo Espichel** and the **farol** (visits Wednesdays 14:00–17:00); the dinosaur
  footprints at **Pedra da Mua** and **Lagosteiros**.
- **Food**: fish landed that morning and grilled whole, and the species genuinely tied to the
  town, **peixe-espada-preto** — Sesimbra is the main mainland port for it. A canning industry
  flourished here from the late 19th into the early 20th century.
- **Everyday**: **4 May**, the Senhor Jesus das Chagas, is the municipal holiday and the day the
  town belongs to itself. Sesimbra is in Carris Metropolitana **Área 3** with Almada and Seixal;
  there is no train.

### Palmela

**68 856 residents (2021)**, spread across a ridge of vineyards with the castle on top of it.
The **Ordem de Santiago** received Palmela from the crown in **1186** and made it their
headquarters; the **Igreja de Santiago** inside the walls was built under D. João I.

- **Landmarks**: the **Castelo de Palmela** at **240 m**, National Monument since 1910, with a
  **pousada** in the old convent buildings since 1945 and a view that reaches Lisbon on a clear
  day; the neolithic and chalcolithic rock-cut caves at **Casal do Pardo / Covas da Moura**,
  Quinta do Anjo.
- **Food**: **Palmela DOC** wine and **Moscatel de Setúbal**; rabbit in the regional style;
  **cabrito assado**; **fogaças** and **suspiros**. **Quinta do Anjo**, in this concelho, is
  where most **queijo de Azeitão** is actually made, and it holds the **Festival do Queijo, Pão
  e Vinho** each April.
- **Everyday**: Fertagus stops at **Pinhal Novo**, **Venda do Alcaide** and **Palmela**, which
  makes the concelho a commuter belt as well as farmland. The **Festa das Vindimas** in
  August/September is the year's landmark. The concelho is also home to the **Volkswagen
  Autoeuropa** car plant *(referenced in Palmela's own municipal bibliography; scale and exact
  siting were not independently verified here)*.

### Azeitão

Not a concelho but a **freguesia of Setúbal**, formed in 2013 from the old parishes of **São
Lourenço** and **São Simão**, and containing **Vila Nogueira de Azeitão** and **Vila Fresca de
Azeitão**. It sits in the lap of the Arrábida and is, in food terms, the most famous square
kilometre on the peninsula.

- **Landmarks**: the **Palácio da Bacalhôa** and **Quinta da Bacalhôa**, with its important
  15th–16th-century azulejo collection; the **Palácio dos Duques de Aveiro**; **Quinta das
  Torres**, a 16th-century noble house; and the **Convento da Arrábida** up on the seaward slope
  above.
- **Food**: **queijo de Azeitão DOP**, **torta de Azeitão**, and **Moscatel de Setúbal** — the
  demarcated region is centred here. The two verified adegas, **José Maria da Fonseca** (1834)
  and **Bacalhôa** (1922), are both open for booked visits.
- **Everyday**: the **mercado mensal** on the **first Sunday of the month** at Brejos de
  Azeitão, and the **Mercado de Azeitão** — which doubles as one of the free park-and-ride
  points for the Arrábida beach shuttle in summer. Azeitão faience
  azulejos are still made here by traditional methods. Bus line **4470** runs Setúbal–Creiro
  through the village.

## Prompt context

The learner lives on the south bank of the Tejo — the Península de Setúbal, which residents
call a Margem Sul or a outra banda, never Lisbon. Use real places: Almada, Cacilhas, Costa da
Caparica, Seixal, Barreiro, Moita, Montijo, Alcochete, Palmela, Azeitão, Sesimbra, Setúbal, the
Serra da Arrábida. Daily life turns on crossing the river: o cacilheiro from Cacilhas to Cais
do Sodré, boats from Seixal, Barreiro and Montijo, the Fertagus train over the lower deck of a
ponte 25 de Abril, the Metro Sul do Tejo in Almada and Seixal, Carris Metropolitana buses, o
passe Navegante. Errands: the Mercado do Livramento in Setúbal, o mercado municipal, Continente
or Pingo Doce, o centro de saúde, a Câmara, a Junta de Freguesia, the school run. Food: choco
frito, peixe-espada from Sesimbra, caldeirada à fragateiro, queijo de Azeitão, torta de Azeitão,
moscatel de Setúbal, uma bica, uma imperial. Small talk: a nortada and cold water at Caparica,
calm clear water at Galapos and Portinho, os
golfinhos in the Sado, a arriba fóssil, the summer beach shuttles. The industrial past — a CUF
in Barreiro, o estaleiro at Margueira, cork in Seixal — is one generation back and still spoken
of.

**Closed since the last pass.** The Setúbal–Tróia operator is **Atlantic Ferries** (confirmed
against the operator's own site); the Azeitão monthly market is the **first Sunday**; the
Mercado do Livramento runs **Tuesday–Sunday, closed Monday** (mun-setubal.pt); and the MST line
termini previously listed here were wrong and have been removed.

**A naming note for consistency across the corpus.** The system is **Metro Sul do Tejo (MST)**,
which is what residents say and what this file uses. **MTS (Metro Transportes do Sul)** is the
operating company. Both are correct; do not present them as two different networks.
