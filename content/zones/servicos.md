# Serviços e burocracia em Portugal

## The institutions

### Finanças / Autoridade Tributária

The tax authority — full name **Autoridade Tributária e Aduaneira (AT)**, portal at portaldasfinancas.gov.pt. It runs income tax (**IRS**), property tax (**IMI**), VAT (**IVA**), and above all issues and manages the **NIF**, the number that every other institution in this file will ask for. A resident deals with Finanças on arrival (getting a NIF is usually the very first bureaucratic act in Portugal, before a bank account or a rental contract can even be signed), every spring when **IRS** is filed (roughly April–June), and again when the **IMI** instalments fall due: a bill of €100 or less is paid once in May; €100–€500 splits into May and November; above €500 it splits three ways, May, August and November.

**Representante fiscal.** This does *not* apply to someone living in Portugal — it is a rule about **non-residents**. Getting a NIF as a non-resident with an address outside the EU/EEA does not by itself require a fiscal representative. The obligation is triggered by a tax relationship afterwards: owning a vehicle or property here, taking an employment contract, or working self-employed. Even then it can be avoided by signing up for electronic notifications (the Portal das Finanças notification system or ViaCTT) — except for self-employed activity subject to IVA, which still needs a VAT representative resident in Portugal. Anyone actually moving to Portugal and registering a Portuguese address is outside this rule entirely.

You bring: passport or Cartão de Cidadão, proof of address. There are two distinct remote channels and it is worth not confusing them: the **Centro de Atendimento Telefónico (CAT)** is the phone line, **217 206 707**, weekdays 9h–19h; the **e-balcão** is the *written* desk inside the Portal das Finanças, where you open a ticket under a subject heading and get an answer in writing you can keep.

Key vocabulary:
- `NIF` — tax number, also called *número de contribuinte*
- `IRS` — personal income tax
- `IMI` — municipal property tax
- `IVA` — VAT
- `e-balcão` — the online/phone help desk
- `representante fiscal` — fiscal representative (for some non-residents)

### Segurança Social

Social Security — seg-social.pt — collects contributions from work and pays out pensions and **subsídios**: unemployment (*subsídio de desemprego*), sickness, parental leave, family allowance (*abono de família*), disability. A resident deals with it when starting a job (the employer registers you, or you self-register), when applying for any of the above subsídios, or when requesting a **NISS**, the Social Security number. The phone line is **300 502 502** (or **210 545 400**, useful from a foreign line), staffed by a person weekdays 9h–18h, with the automated service running 24/7; have your NISS and telephone access code ready before calling.

You bring: NIF, ID, and (for work-related matters) your contract details.

Key vocabulary:
- `NISS` — Social Security number
- `subsídio` — benefit/allowance
- `subsídio de desemprego` — unemployment benefit
- `abono de família` — child benefit
- `entidade patronal` — employer
- `descontos` — social security contributions ("deductions") taken from a payslip

### SNS and the Centro de Saúde

The **Serviço Nacional de Saúde (SNS)**, Portugal's public health service, has existed since 1979 (Law 56/79) and is funded through taxation. The **Centro de Saúde** is the local, first point of contact — where a resident registers, is assigned a **número de utente**, and (subject to availability) a **médico de família**, a family doctor. **SNS 24** (sns24.gov.pt, phone **808 24 24 24**, free) is the national triage line and portal. Clinical triage and advice run **24 hours a day, every day**; the non-clinical, administrative side of the line runs **8h–22h**. It is also the number to ring when you do not know which centro de saúde covers your address, and it can book appointments. Registration at a centro de saúde and assignment to a doctor's list are technically two separate steps — you can be registered and still be waitlisted for a doctor, but you keep the right to care in the meantime.

You bring: passport or Cartão de Cidadão, título de residência if a foreign national, and a comprovativo de morada for your area.

Key vocabulary:
- `número de utente` — SNS user number
- `centro de saúde` — health centre (primary care)
- `médico de família` — family doctor
- `marcação` / `consulta` — appointment / medical appointment
- `receita` — prescription
- `urgência` — A&E / emergency department

### AIMA (formerly SEF)

**Since October 2023, immigration in Portugal is not SEF.** SEF (Serviço de Estrangeiros e Fronteiras), which had handled foreign residents and border control since 1986, was extinguished by **Decreto-Lei n.º 41/2023, de 2 de junho**, which created the **Agência para a Integração, Migrações e Asilo (AIMA)** and took effect on **29 October 2023**. AIMA absorbed SEF's residency and integration functions along with the old Alto Comissariado para as Migrações; SEF's police duties were split between the **PSP, the GNR and the Polícia Judiciária**. Any material still referring to "SEF" for residence-permit matters — including a lot of what a language model or an old webpage will confidently tell you — is out of date.

AIMA issues and renews the **título de residência** (residence permit) for non-EU/EEA nationals, handles family reunification, and processes asylum. This is the office with the worst backlog in the system: first-time issuance has a legal deadline of 90 working days, family-reunification cases can legally run to nine months, and getting an in-person appointment at all is often the actual bottleneck people describe. AIMA runs an online **Portal de Renovações** for permits nearing expiry, opened in monthly cycles by expiry date, so many renewals no longer need a visit at all — but first applications and biometric data collection generally still do. The general contact line is **217 115 000** (landline) or **+351 965 903 700** (mobile), weekdays 8h–20h, email geral@aima.gov.pt, plus the online contact form at contactenos.aima.gov.pt. Portuguese consular guidance points people at those phone numbers for booking appointments at AIMA's own desks, so treat the line as a scheduling channel and not only an information one. The practical constraint is not which channel you use but that in-person slots are opened in waves rather than on demand.

You bring: passport, proof of the underlying visa/right to reside, comprovativo de morada (property documents are increasingly requested, not just a utility bill — confirm current requirements before an appointment).

Key vocabulary:
- `título de residência` — residence permit/card
- `autorização de residência` — residence authorisation (the underlying legal status)
- `agendamento` / `marcação` — booking an appointment
- `dados biométricos` — biometric data (fingerprints, photo)
- `reagrupamento familiar` — family reunification
- `prazo` — deadline/processing time

### Junta de Freguesia

The **freguesia** is the smallest unit of local government (a parish, though the word has no real religious weight anymore), and the **junta de freguesia** is its elected executive — a president plus *vogais* (board members). It is the office for small, local, everyday paperwork: most usefully, the **atestado de residência**, a stamped certificate proving you live at an address, issued within about three business days of asking, and often the only proof of address a newcomer can get before their name is on a lease or a utility bill. Also handled here: various *certidões* and declarations, dog/pet registration, and civil matters delegated from the câmara.

You bring: Cartão de Cidadão or título de residência, and — if you have it — a rental contract or utility bill; if you don't, a Junta can sometimes issue the atestado on the word of two witnesses who confirm you live there (unverified in detail — practice varies by freguesia).

Key vocabulary:
- `freguesia` — parish (the local government unit and the area itself)
- `atestado de residência` — residence certificate
- `presidente da junta` — the parish president
- `certidão` — official certificate/record extract

### Câmara Municipal

The **município**'s executive body, one level up from the freguesia — the town/city hall. This is where property-related municipal business happens: building permits, local taxes and fees, waste and recycling, urban planning, and the municipality's own **Loja do Munícipe** or **Gabinete de Atendimento ao Munícipe (GAM)**, a front desk that triages requests and complaints across all the câmara's departments. A resident typically shows up here for anything involving a building, a licence, or a complaint about the street outside their door.

Key vocabulary:
- `câmara municipal` — city/town council
- `munícipe` — resident of the municipality, in the formal sense used at the counter
- `licença` — permit/licence
- `taxa` — municipal fee
- `Loja do Munícipe` — the câmara's citizen service desk

### Loja do Cidadão

A physical one-stop shop where several institutions share a building and a queueing system, so one visit can cover Finanças, Segurança Social, the Cartão de Cidadão/passport desk (IRN), IMT driving-licence matters, and even utility companies like EDP or CTT under a single roof. The nationwide network — now branded **Espaço Cidadão**, with well over 900 locations including câmaras, juntas de freguesia and public libraries as well as the flagship Lojas de Cidadão — means a small town's local desk may offer only a handful of these services, while a full Loja do Cidadão in a city offers most of them. A digital equivalent, the **Loja de Cidadão Virtual**, gives assisted service by video call without travelling anywhere.

Key vocabulary:
- `Espaço Cidadão` — the current umbrella name for these citizen-service desks
- `Loja do Cidadão` — the original, larger multi-service branch
- `balcão único` — single/one-stop counter
- `atendimento` — service/being attended to at a counter

### Conservatória do Registo Civil

The civil registry office, part of the **Instituto dos Registos e do Notariado (IRN)** under the Ministry of Justice. It records the events that define a legal life: birth, marriage, death, adoption, name changes, nationality. Most **certidões** (birth, marriage, death certificates) can now be requested without a visit through the **Civil Online** portal (civilonline.mj.pt). Marriage itself still starts here in person, with a *declaração de intenção de casar* that opens the legal process before any ceremony.

Key vocabulary:
- `conservatória` — registry office (from *conservar*, to keep/preserve records)
- `certidão de nascimento / casamento / óbito` — birth / marriage / death certificate
- `registo civil` — civil registration
- `declaração de intenção de casar` — declaration of intent to marry

### IMT

The **Instituto da Mobilidade e dos Transportes** handles driving licences (**carta de condução**) and vehicles: registration (**matrícula**) and the compulsory roadworthiness inspection (**Inspeção Periódica Obrigatória, IPO**). A first category-B licence needs a minimum age of 18 (17 with supervised driving, *condução acompanhada*), and the whole process runs through an approved driving school, which handles the IMT paperwork: theory exam (*exame de código*), a minimum of practical hours, then the practical exam; the physical card typically arrives by post 5–10 working days after passing. For a light passenger car the IPO falls due **at four years old, then at six, then at eight, and every year from then on**, and it is keyed to the **month of the first registration** — the month on the matrícula is the deadline, not a date you choose. Driving a vehicle without a valid inspection is punishable by a fine of roughly **€250 to €1,250**; simply not carrying the inspection slip is a much smaller matter (about €60, halved if you produce the document within eight days). Figures move — treat these as the order of magnitude, not a quote. Inspections happen at any **CITV** (Centro de Inspeção Técnica de Veículos), of which there are over 200 across mainland Portugal. Most of this — booking exams, renewing a licence, checking IPO status — can be done at servicos.imt-ip.pt, IMT Online.

Key vocabulary:
- `carta de condução` — driving licence
- `matrícula` — vehicle registration / number plate
- `Inspeção Periódica Obrigatória (IPO)` — compulsory vehicle inspection
- `código` — the theory exam
- `exame prático` — the practical driving test
- `condução acompanhada` — supervised driving (age 17)

### CTT

**CTT — Correios de Portugal** (ctt.pt) is the post office, but its counters do far more than stamps and parcels. **Carta registada** — registered mail requiring a signature — is how official notices from Finanças or AIMA often arrive, which makes checking the letterbox a genuine bureaucratic act: missing a registered letter can mean missing a legal deadline. CTT counters and the affiliated **Payshop** agent network (found in many small shops, not just post offices) take bill payments by reference, and CTT also runs its own bank, **Banco CTT**.

Key vocabulary:
- `carta registada` — registered/recorded-delivery letter
- `aviso de receção` — return receipt (proof of delivery)
- `Payshop` — the bill-payment agent network
- `encomenda` — parcel
- `selo` — stamp

### The utilities (EDP, Águas, MEO/NOS/Vodafone)

**EDP** is the dominant electricity (and gas) supplier, though others exist. Putting a contract in your own name (*mudança de titularidade*) needs the new holder's ID, the meter's identifying code — **CPE** for electricity, **CUI** for gas — a comprovativo de morada, and an IBAN if paying by direct debit; it's free and typically takes 2–3 business days for electricity, up to about a week where water is involved. **Water** is supplied by whichever municipal or regional company covers that concelho (Lisbon's is EPAL; every area is different), and the same kind of titularidade process applies, at a similar pace. For mobile/TV/broadband, the big three are **MEO**, **NOS**, and **Vodafone**: contracts commonly carry a 24-month **fidelização** (loyalty/lock-in period), which by law can only be imposed with your explicit agreement in exchange for a genuine benefit — a discount or subsidised equipment, not just a broken router being swapped for a working one. Plans without fidelização exist, cost more monthly, and can be cancelled any time without penalty.

Key vocabulary:
- `titularidade` — the named account holder on a contract
- `CPE` / `CUI` — the electricity / gas meter identifier codes
- `fidelização` — the minimum-term lock-in on a contract
- `fatura` — bill/invoice
- `contador` — meter

## The documents

**NIF** — *Número de Identificação Fiscal*, nine digits (eight sequential plus a check digit), issued by Finanças. Individuals' NIFs conventionally start with 1–3. It is asked for constantly, far beyond tax matters — opening a bank account, signing a lease, a phone contract, sometimes even a gym membership — because it is Portugal's default way of attaching a transaction to a person. Everyone calls it *o contribuinte* in speech as much as *o NIF*.

**NISS** — *Número de Identificação da Segurança Social*, eleven digits. Anyone with a Cartão de Cidadão already has one printed on the back of the card; foreign nationals without a CC apply through the Segurança Social Direta portal, attaching ID documents, and then receive a registered letter telling them where to collect it — the whole process usually takes 15–30 working days.

**Cartão de Cidadão (CC)** — the citizen card, issued since 2008, folding what used to be four separate documents (the old ID card, the NIF card, the NISS/Social Security card, the SNS user card) plus the electoral card into one chip card with digital certificates for authentication and electronic signature. Valid ten years over the age of 25, five years under it, and the fee follows that validity rather than the age directly: a standard counter renewal costs **€18** for the ten-year card and **€15** for the five-year one. Urgent turnaround costs roughly double, and renewing online is cheaper than renewing at a counter. Issued and renewed through the IRN network — Conservatórias, Lojas/Espaços Cidadão.

**Número de Utente** — the SNS health-service user number. Automatic (and printed on the CC) for citizens; foreign residents without a Cartão de Cidadão request it directly at their local Centro de Saúde.

**Título de Residência** — the biometric residence card AIMA issues to non-EU/EEA nationals living legally in Portugal. It is the document that proves the right to reside, and its renewal cycle (via the online Portal de Renovações or an in-person appointment) is one of the most-discussed pieces of Portuguese bureaucracy among immigrants, precisely because of the waits described above.

**Atestado de Residência** — the Junta de Freguesia's stamped statement of where you live, useful as a substitute proof of address when nothing else in your name yet exists (before a lease, before a utility bill).

**Comprovativo de morada** — the general category "proof of address": a utility bill less than three months old, a signed rental contract, the atestado de residência above, or an official domicile certificate pulled from the Finanças portal. Needed to get a NIF, a NISS, open a bank account, and enrol in almost anything.

## Money and paying for things

**Multibanco** is both the ATM network and the payment brand behind almost every card transaction in the country — the little yellow-and-blue logo. Paying a bill by **referência de pagamento** means keying in three numbers at any Multibanco machine or on a banking app: **Entidade** (who's being paid), **Referência** (which bill), **Montante** (how much) — used for everything from a water bill to an IMI instalment, which is why Portuguese taxes get paid at a cash machine as often as online.

**MB Way** is the mobile layer on top of Multibanco: send or request money by phone number, pay in shops and online, generate a disposable **MB Net** virtual card number, even withdraw cash from an ATM without the physical card. It can be linked to a debit or a credit card, and the fee structure differs slightly depending which — debit/immediate transfer is capped lower than a credit-card-funded payment.

**Débito direto** (direct debit) is how most people pay recurring bills — electricity, water, phone — and needs only an IBAN to set up once.

The everyday distinction that trips learners up: **cartão multibanco** is a debit card, drawing straight from a current account, and it is the default, near-universal way to pay for anything; a **cartão de crédito** is a separate, secondary thing many residents don't bother with day-to-day.

And then there's the ritual repeated at every till in the country: **"Tem contribuinte?"** — do you have a tax number [to put on this receipt]? Giving your NIF means the sale is recorded against your name at Finanças, which lets you deduct certain categories of spending (health, education, gyms, hairdressers, restaurants) from IRS. There used to be a second incentive, the **Fatura da Sorte** lottery, which allocated draw entries against invoices carrying your NIF; **the draws have been suspended since January 2024**, with the government announcing a relaunch in a reworked format aimed at sectors where people rarely ask for an invoice. Do not tell anyone they are entering a lottery until it is actually running again. The deduction, and the habit, remain. Businesses are legally required to issue a fatura for every transaction whether or not it's asked for.

## Phrases that actually get things done

**Arriving at a counter**
- *Boa tarde, vim tratar de um assunto do NIF.* — Good afternoon, I've come to sort out something about my NIF.
- *Preciso de marcar uma consulta / um atendimento.* — I need to book an appointment / a service visit.
- *É aqui que se trata das renovações?* — Is this where renewals are handled?
- *Desculpe, é a primeira vez que cá venho — pode ajudar-me?* — Sorry, this is my first time here — can you help me?

**Taking a senha**
- *Onde é que se tira a senha?* — Where do you take a ticket?
- *Qual é o número que está a chamar?* — What number is it calling now?
- *Já tirei senha, estou a aguardar.* — I've already taken a ticket, I'm waiting.
- *Perdi a minha vez, posso tirar outra senha?* — I missed my turn, can I take another ticket?

**Explaining a problem**
- *Recebi uma carta e não percebi bem o que preciso de fazer.* — I got a letter and I didn't quite understand what I need to do.
- *Falta-me um documento — o que é que posso trazer em vez disso?* — I'm missing a document — what can I bring instead?
- *O meu processo está parado há semanas, sabe porquê?* — My case has been stuck for weeks, do you know why?
- *Ainda não recebi resposta ao meu pedido.* — I still haven't had a reply to my request.

**Asking someone to repeat**
- *Desculpe, pode repetir mais devagar, se faz favor?* — Sorry, could you repeat that more slowly, please?
- *Não percebi bem — pode explicar outra vez?* — I didn't quite understand — could you explain again?
- *Pode escrever isso, se faz favor?* — Could you write that down, please?
- *Fala inglês, por acaso?* — Do you happen to speak English? (worth knowing, though the goal is not needing it)

**Making an appointment (marcação)**
- *Queria marcar uma consulta com o médico de família.* — I'd like to book an appointment with my family doctor.
- *Há vaga para esta semana ou só para o mês que vem?* — Is there availability this week or only next month?
- *Posso remarcar para outro dia?* — Can I reschedule for another day?
- *A marcação é só online ou também dá para ser por telefone?* — Is booking only online, or can it also be done by phone?

## Everyday rituals and their humour

The **senha** — the little paper ticket from a machine by the door — turns a public office into a lottery of numbers and a wall-mounted display everyone stares at. It has been quietly digitised in places (apps that let you pull a senha before leaving the house so you arrive closer to your number), but the fundamental genre of person — standing, staring at a red LED counter, willing it upward by force of attention — hasn't gone anywhere.

**"Tem contribuinte?"** is asked at every till in the country, from the supermarket to the café, and answered on reflex by residents with a nine-digit number recited faster than their own phone number. Visitors who don't yet have a NIF get a slightly pitying look and a receipt with no name on it.

The **horário** — opening hours — of public counters still routinely closes for lunch, so a trip to sort out a document can be quietly derailed by arriving at 12:45 and finding the shutter down until early afternoon; asking a Portuguese friend "is it open now?" gets a shrug that means *technically, but check*.

And the phone menus: dial the SNS 24 line, the Segurança Social line, or the Finanças CAT, and the pleasant recorded voice will offer a numbered list of options in which the one you actually need is reliably third from last. None of this is a complaint about the people behind the counters, who are generally doing their best with a system that runs on paper trails and stamps; it's the system's own sense of humour, and residents learn to laugh at it rather than with it.

## Prompt context

The learner is dealing with everyday Portuguese bureaucracy: getting a NIF at Finanças before anything else can happen, a NISS from Segurança Social, registering at the Centro de Saúde for a número de utente and a médico de família through SNS 24, and — if a non-EU national — navigating AIMA (the agency that replaced SEF in October 2023) for a título de residência, notorious for long waits and for in-person slots that open in waves rather than on demand. The Junta de Freguesia issues an atestado de residência when no other comprovativo de morada exists yet; the Câmara Municipal handles property and local permits; a Loja do Cidadão or Espaço Cidadão bundles several of these services under one roof; the Conservatória do Registo Civil handles certidões; IMT handles a carta de condução and vehicle IPO; CTT delivers the carta registada that carries official notices and takes bill payments via Payshop; EDP, água, and MEO/NOS/Vodafone all require comprovativo de morada and an IBAN to set up. Money moves through Multibanco referências (Entidade, Referência, Montante), MB Way, and débito direto, and every till asks "Tem contribuinte?" Situations to build: taking a senha and waiting to be called, explaining a missing document, asking someone to repeat or slow down, booking or rescheduling a marcação, and the particular dread of an unopened carta registada. Tone: dry, affectionate, never mocking the staff — the bureaucracy is the joke, not the people running it. European Portuguese only, tu register.
