# Transportes e mercados

## Trains — CP

**Comboios de Portugal (CP)** runs five tiers of passenger service, and knowing which
one you are boarding tells you whether you need a reservation.

- **Urbanos** — subsidised commuter trains in and around Lisboa, Porto and Coimbra.
  Frequent stops, single class, no reserved seats, lowest fares.
- **Regional** — "o serviço mais básico da CP": stops everywhere, including small
  unstaffed platforms, and is how the smaller towns off the main lines stay connected.
- **InterRegional** — a step up, running direct or semi-direct between regional
  centres (used on the Norte, Minho and Douro lines among others).
- **Intercidades (IC)** — the fast service between the country's main cities, up to
  200 km/h, locomotive-hauled, two classes, **reserved seating**, and a bar car. Only
  Intercidades is covered by the state's Contrato de Serviço Público.
- **Alfa Pendular (AP)** — CP's premium tilting train, up to 220 km/h, two classes
  (**Conforto** / **Turista**), full onboard service.

**Reservations.** On Urbanos, Regional and InterRegional trains you buy a plain
**bilhete** and sit wherever there's room — no reservation exists or is needed. On
**Alfa Pendular and Intercidades, seat reservation (lugar marcado) is mandatory**: the
ticket you buy already carries a seat and carriage number, and it needs to be bought
in advance (online, in the app, at a station machine, or at the counter) rather than
picked up on board.

**Bilhete vs. passe.** A *bilhete* is a ticket for one journey. A *passe* is a season
pass for repeated travel. Since **21 October 2024**, CP has sold a **Passe Ferroviário
Verde** at **20 €/month**, valid on every CP service *except* Alfa Pendular (and
separate from the city passes below) — a flat-rate option for anyone commuting by
train regularly. *(Price correct for 2026; fares and passes change — check cp.pt
before relying on a figure.)*

**The lines that matter most:**

- **Linha do Norte** — Lisboa (Santa Apolónia / Oriente) to Porto (Campanhã), **336
  km**, the country's main railway artery. Alfa Pendular, Intercidades, Regional and
  Urbano services all run sections of it; it passes through Vila Nova de Gaia,
  Espinho, Ovar, Aveiro and Coimbra.
- **Linha do Douro** — Ermesinde to Pocinho, about **160 km**, built 1873–1887,
  following the Douro valley the whole way. Widely called *a linha mais bela de
  Portugal*; heritage steam services run on sections of it in summer. The onward leg
  to Barca d'Alva closed in **1988** and has not reopened.
- **Linha do Oeste** — serves Torres Vedras, Bombarral, Óbidos and Caldas da Rainha,
  but **not** Peniche, Ericeira or Nazaré, which have no railway at all and depend on
  road transport.
- **Linha do Algarve** — Lagos to Vila Real de Santo António, **139.5 km**, calling at
  Lagos, Portimão, Tunes (the junction for Faro-bound trains), Albufeira, Faro,
  Olhão, Tavira and VRSA. Alfa Pendular and Intercidades run between Faro and Tunes;
  Regional trains cover the whole line.

## City transport

**Lisboa.** The **Metropolitano de Lisboa** has four lines, always called by colour,
never by number: **Azul** (Santa Apolónia ↔ Reboleira), **Amarela** (Rato ↔
Odivelas), **Verde** (Cais do Sodré ↔ Telheiras), and **Vermelha** (São Sebastião ↔
Aeroporto Humberto Delgado, via Saldanha, Alameda and Oriente). 56 stations across
roughly 44 km. **Carris** runs the buses, the historic **eléctricos** — tram 28
(Graça–Estrela via Alfama and the Baixa) and tram 15E out to Belém are the two anyone
mentions — and the funiculars/**ascensores** that climb the steepest streets (Glória,
Bica, Lavra). The **cacilheiros** — the Tejo ferries — are run by **Transtejo/Soflusa
(TTSL)**: Cais do Sodré↔Cacilhas, Terreiro do Paço↔Barreiro, Montijo↔Cais do Sodré,
Seixal↔Cais do Sodré, and a newer Trafaria–Porto Brandão–Pedrouços/Algés route.

Fares run on the **Navegante** system. For occasional trips there's the **cartão
Navegante ocasional** (a 0,50 € reloadable card, valid a year, topped up with a single
ticket, a 24-hour ticket, or pay-as-you-go **Zapping** credit). For regular travel
there's the **passe Navegante**: **Municipal** (one concelho) or **Metropolitano**
(the whole Área Metropolitana de Lisboa), plus a discounted **Navegante +65**; it's
free for anyone up to 23 inclusive, student or not. One card, one validation, works
across metro, Carris, CP urban trains and the ferries. *(Figures correct for 2026 —
these prices are reviewed yearly.)*

**Porto.** The **Metro do Porto** runs as a light-rail network — several lines share
the same central trunk track and branch apart further out, which is why two different
letters can arrive at the same platform. Six lines are running: **A/Azul**
(Senhor de Matosinhos ↔ Estádio do Dragão), **B/Vermelha** (Póvoa de Varzim ↔
Estádio do Dragão), **C/Verde** (ISMAI ↔ Campanhã), **D/Amarela** (Hospital São
João ↔ Vila d'Este), **E/Violeta** (Aeroporto ↔ Trindade) and **F/Laranja** (Senhora
da Hora ↔ Fânzeres); two more, **G** and **H**, are under construction. **STCP** runs
the city's buses, plus a handful of heritage tram lines aimed mostly at visitors.
Fares across Metro do Porto, STCP and CP's urban Porto trains run on **Andante**: a
zone system where a ticket is valid for a ring of zones counted outward from wherever
you first validate it. The minimum is **Z2** (your zone plus the ring around it); a
**Z3** ticket reaches one ring further. *(Z2/Z3 fares change yearly — check
andante.pt rather than assuming a number.)*

## Coaches and regional buses

**Rede Expressos** is not a single company but a national brand shared by several
coach operators — Transdev Portugal, Rodoviária do Tejo, Barraqueiro Transportes and
others — descended from the intercity network Rodoviária Nacional started in the late
1970s. Between them they claim several hundred destinations reachable multiple times
a day, filling in almost everywhere CP doesn't run.

**FlixBus**, the German long-distance operator, has been expanding aggressively in
Portugal and now competes head-on with Rede Expressos, including a public dispute
over shared access to Lisboa's Sete Rios terminal (a court ordered Rede Expressos to
let FlixBus use it; FlixBus has mostly operated out of Gare do Oriente in the
meantime). About four in five FlixBus trips within Portugal stay domestic; the rest
run on to Spain, France and Italy.

Away from the main coach routes, rural Portugal runs on **carreiras** — local buses
that may pass a village once or twice a day, often timed around the school run or the
market, and thin out sharply (or vanish) on Sundays. Knowing the one bus a day exists,
and when, is real local knowledge in places the train never reached.

## Driving

**Via Verde** is Portugal's electronic toll tag: a DSRC transponder stuck to the
windscreen talks to an antenna over the tolled lane as you pass, and the toll is
debited straight from your bank account — no barrier, no stopping, and the lanes work
even well above ordinary speed limits.

**Ex-SCUT motorways.** A batch of motorways — the **Costa de Prata**, **Grande
Porto**, **Norte Litoral**, **Algarve (A22)**, **Beira Interior (A23)**, **Interior
Norte (A24)** and **Beira Litoral/Beira Alta (A25)** concessions — were built under
the **SCUT** model (*Sem Custos para o Utilizador*): free to drivers, paid for by the
state on a shadow-toll basis. Real tolls were introduced on Costa de Prata, Grande
Porto and Norte Litoral in October 2010, and on the rest in December 2011 — hugely
unpopular, and blamed by some studies for business closures in the interior. **Since 1
January 2025**, tolls have been abolished again on the interior and Algarve
ex-SCUT roads (broadly: A4 Transmontana and the Marão tunnel, A13/A13-1 Pinhal
Interior, A22, A23, A24, A25, and part of A28), while **Costa de Prata, Grande Porto
and the rest of Norte Litoral remain tolled**. *(This is a live policy area — confirm
current status before a trip.)*

**No Via Verde device?** These roads have no toll booths at all — payment is
electronic-only. You have roughly two weeks (commonly cited as 15 business days) after
passing to pay at a **CTT** post office or a **Payshop** agent, or online, and a small
per-passage surcharge applies (on the order of 0,32 € per passage, capped). Rental
cars usually come with their own device or billing arrangement — always confirm with
the rental company before driving on a portagem-only road.

**Parking.** Paid street parking is the **zona azul**: a **disco de estacionamento**
(a cardboard dial, or increasingly a paper *dístico*) set to your arrival time and
left visible on the dashboard, checked by municipal wardens against a posted time
limit — typically an hour and a half to four hours, usually free at night, on Sundays
and on public holidays. Paid meters (**parquímetros**) are common in town centres.

**Fuel.** **Gasóleo** is diesel; **gasolina** is petrol, usually sold as 95 or 98
octane. Ask for a **posto de abastecimento** or **bomba de gasolina**; most stations
are self-service now, though some still have an attendant.

**IMT.** The **Instituto da Mobilidade e dos Transportes** issues the **carta de
condução**. Category B (cars up to 3 500 kg, up to 8 passengers besides the driver)
has a minimum age of 18, or 17 with **condução acompanhada** — supervised by a
qualified driver until turning 18. An EU/EEA licence stays valid to drive on once you
move to Portugal, but residents must register their address with the IMT within 60
days of establishing residency. Non-EU licence holders covered by a bilateral
agreement can drive for up to 185 days without residency; once resident, the licence
must be exchanged for a Portuguese one within 2 years.

## Islands

**Azores.** Inter-island ferries are run by **Atlânticoline**. The core route is the
**Horta–Madalena** crossing between Faial and Pico — about half an hour, running
**year-round**, and used as an everyday commute for work, school and hospital
appointments. Around it, Atlânticoline runs seasonal lines connecting the wider
Central Group: a "Triângulo" service linking Faial, Pico and São Jorge; a dedicated
São Jorge–Pico crossing; and less frequent lines out to Graciosa and Terceira,
typically only on set days and mostly in summer. *(Route names and days shift
season to season — treat any specific weekday as provisional and check
atlanticoline.pt before travelling.)* Bad weather is not a footnote here: crossings
are cancelled outright when the sea is too rough, and — as with SATA flights in fog —
plans routinely carry the qualifier *se o tempo deixar* ("if the weather allows").

**Madeira.** The **Porto Santo Line** ferry — currently the ship **Lobo Marinho** —
connects **Funchal to Porto Santo**, a crossing of roughly two hours. How comfortable
those two hours are depends entirely on the swell; it is also possible to fly (Binter,
about fifteen minutes). Cargo to both archipelagos travels by boat as well as by air,
so a bad sea can mean thinner supermarket shelves before it means anything else.

## Phrases for getting around and buying

**Trains and coaches**

- *Um bilhete para o Porto, se faz favor.* — A ticket to Porto, please.
- *É preciso marcar lugar?* — Do I need to reserve a seat?
- *De que via parte o comboio para Lisboa?* — Which platform does the Lisbon train
  leave from? (*via*, not *cais* — CP boards use *via*; bus terminals use *cais*.)
- *Este lugar está livre?* — Is this seat taken? (asked before sitting, on an
  unreserved train or coach)
- *Desculpe, este autocarro vai para Torres Vedras?* — Excuse me, does this bus go to
  Torres Vedras?
- *A que horas é o próximo para Peniche?* — What time's the next one to Peniche?
- *O comboio vem atrasado?* — Is the train running late?
- *Dá-me um retorno para o Porto, por favor.* — A return to Porto, please.

**At the market**

- *Quanto custa o quilo?* — How much is it a kilo?
- *Dê-me meio quilo de tomate, se faz favor.* — Half a kilo of tomatoes, please.
- *Uma dúzia de ovos.* — A dozen eggs.
- *Leva mais um bocadinho?* — (stallholder, adding a bit extra) Will you take a
  little more? — normal market generosity; you're not expected to refuse.
- *Está fresco isto?* — Is this fresh?
- *Pode ser um mais maduro, para hoje?* — Could I have a riper one, for today?
- *Tem troco para vinte euros?* — Do you have change for twenty euros?
- *É a última que tem?* — Is that the last one you've got?
- *Fica quanto, tudo junto?* — How much altogether?

**Driving and parking**

- *Isto tem portagem?* — Does this road have a toll?
- *Onde ponho o disco?* — Where do I put the parking disc?
- *É gasóleo ou gasolina?* — Diesel or petrol? (asked at a rental counter or pump)

## Prompt context

Sandra should build errands around real mechanisms, not invented timetables. A
learner buying a CP ticket needs to know Alfa Pendular and Intercidades require a
booked seat in advance, while Regional and Urbano trains don't; Lisboa runs on
Navegante (Municipal or Metropolitano passes, or a Zapping-loaded card for occasional
trips) and Porto on Andante's Z2/Z3 zone counting — never invent a fare, since both
change yearly. Coach travel means Rede Expressos or, increasingly, FlixBus, and
rural villages depend on infrequent carreiras rather than a train. Driving examples
should distinguish Via Verde holders from visitors paying an ex-SCUT toll after the
fact at a CTT counter, and should know tolls came off several interior and Algarve
motorways from January 2025. For markets, use only the days verified in this file —
Barcelos on Thursday, Ponte de Lima's fortnightly Monday feira (documented since
1125), Torres Vedras's first-Saturday Feira Rural, Caldas da Rainha's daily Praça da
Fruta — since the family lives near Santa Cruz and Torres Vedras, lean on those two
towns' real rhythms for local colour, and never state a market day for a town not on
that list. Keep prices as mechanisms ("a Navegante pass" rather than "a 40€ pass")
unless quoting one of the figures given here, and note that any figure could be a year
out of date.
